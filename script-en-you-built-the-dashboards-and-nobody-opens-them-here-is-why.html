[0:45-2:00] BEAT 1 — Why does nobody open the dashboards you built? *[MODALITY: CUSTOM ANIMATION of a report with twelve charts, none of them answering a single plain question, then the tab closing]* *[ON-SCREEN CHAPTER CARD: "Why does nobody open it?"]*

CLAIM. *[ON-SCREEN CLAIM CARD]* Most dashboards go unused for one plain reason. They don't answer a question the person actually has when they sit down to work.

PROOF. *[MODALITY: SCREEN-RECORD of a busy report, cursor hunting across charts]* The data team builds around the data it has. Twelve charts, a filter for every field, every metric anyone ever mentioned. Then the person opens it and thinks one thing. Are we ahead or behind, and what do I do about it. The report doesn't say. So they close it and go ask a colleague, the same as before you built anything.

PAYOFF. *[MODALITY: ON-SCREEN TEXT, numbered takeaway 1]* Before you build, write down the single decision the report is meant to drive. If you can't name the decision in one sentence, nobody will open the report.

RE-HOOK. *[TALKING HEAD]* Now say you did answer the right question. People still drift back to their own spreadsheet anyway. Here's why.

[2:00-3:30] BEAT 2 — Why do people trust their own spreadsheet more? *[MODALITY: B-ROLL, someone glancing at a dashboard, then turning to a familiar Excel file they built themselves]* *[ON-SCREEN CHAPTER CARD: "Why the spreadsheet wins"]*

CLAIM. *[ON-SCREEN CLAIM CARD]* People go back to their spreadsheet when the number on your dashboard doesn't match the number they already had.

PROOF. *[MODALITY: DATA-VIZ, two figures side by side, 4.20M and 4.35M, a question mark between them]* Your dashboard says revenue is 4.2 million. Their close file says 4.35. In the meeting nobody can say which one is right, so they use the one they built and understand. That happens twice and the dashboard is done. The research backs this up. On average only about a quarter of the people a company gives BI tools to ever use them, and that number has barely moved in seven years, per BARC and Eckerson Group in 2022. The tools are fine. The reason sits underneath, in the numbers.

PAYOFF. *[MODALITY: ON-SCREEN TEXT, numbered takeaway 2]* Before you ship, reconcile your headline number to the one finance already trusts, down to the cent. If it doesn't tie out, fix that first. A report that argues with the number people know is worse than no report.

RE-HOOK. *[TALKING HEAD]* That covers the number itself. Next is the mistake that gets baked in before you write a single line.

[3:30-5:00] BEAT 3 — Did you build the report they asked for, or the one you wanted to build? *[MODALITY: CUSTOM ANIMATION, a vague one-line request on one side, an elaborate self-serve tool with twenty controls on the other]* *[ON-SCREEN CHAPTER CARD: "The wrong report, built well"]*

CLAIM. *[ON-SCREEN CLAIM CARD]* A lot of unused dashboards are technically excellent and completely beside the point.

PROOF. *[MODALITY: SCREEN-RECORD, a report loaded with slicers and drill-throughs]* The team gets a vague ask and fills the gap with whatever is interesting in the data. A filter for every field. A drill-through on everything. But the person wanted one view. This month against plan, by team, with the three biggest gaps called out at the top. Instead they got a self-serve tool and a pile of homework, so they never came back.

PAYOFF. *[MODALITY: ON-SCREEN TEXT, numbered takeaway 3]* Show a rough version to the real user in week one, before you polish anything. If they don't lean in, the topic is wrong, and no amount of polish saves it later.

RE-HOOK. *[TALKING HEAD]* So some of your dashboards are worth saving and some aren't. How do you tell which, and what do you do with each one?

[5:00-6:30] BEAT 4 — What carries over when you fix it, and what do you rebuild? *[MODALITY: CUSTOM ANIMATION, a stack with a solid base labelled "data and model — keep" and a thin top layer labelled "the view people see — rebuild"]* *[ON-SCREEN CHAPTER CARD: "Keep vs rebuild"]*

CLAIM. *[ON-SCREEN CLAIM CARD]* When you fix a dashboard nobody opens, most of the real work carries over. What you rebuild is usually the smallest part.

PROOF. *[MODALITY: SCREEN-RECORD, showing the model and relationships underneath a report, then the visuals on top]* What carries over is the clean data, the relationships, the trusted number underneath, the refresh that already runs. That's the expensive part, and it's usually fine. What you rebuild is the front. The questions it answers, the layout, the one clear takeaway. People assume a rebuild means starting from zero, so they never start. It doesn't mean that at all. You're keeping the engine and reshaping the dashboard on top of it.

PAYOFF. *[MODALITY: ON-SCREEN TEXT, numbered takeaway 4]* Separate the model from the view. Keep the model, rebuild the view around one decision. That's two days of work, not a rebuild from the ground up.

RE-HOOK. *[TALKING HEAD]* There's a catch. The moment you touch a live report, you can make the whole thing worse. Here's the real risk.

[6:30-8:00] BEAT 5 — What can go wrong the moment you touch a live report? *[MODALITY: DATA-VIZ, a quarter total shifting from one figure to another with no warning, an exec's face turning]* *[ON-SCREEN CHAPTER CARD: "The risk when you touch it"]*

CLAIM. *[ON-SCREEN CLAIM CARD]* The biggest risk in fixing a dashboard is changing the number while you change the look, so now two versions disagree.

PROOF. *[MODALITY: SCREEN-RECORD then B-ROLL of a review meeting]* You rebuild the view, adjust one filter, and last quarter's total moves by a percent. To you it's a rounding change. To the CFO who quoted the old figure to leadership, the data team just rewrote history, and trust drops again, this time harder to earn back. Two more ways it goes sideways. You point the report at a new source and the refresh times out on the morning of the review, so the numbers are stale. Or you rename a few fields and three other reports go blank because they depended on the old names. *[ON-SCREEN CARD listing the three risks: "the number moves silently / the refresh times out / other reports break"]*

PAYOFF. *[MODALITY: ON-SCREEN TEXT, numbered takeaway 5]* Version every change. Keep the old numbers reproducible, write down what moved and why, and tell the people who use the report before they spot it themselves. Changing a number without a word is how you lose a room.

RE-HOOK. *[TALKING HEAD]* So how do you actually run all this without breaking trust? Let me walk you through a real one, and the change I promised at the start.

[8:00-9:15] BEAT 6 — A real example: the report that got opened again *[MODALITY: B-ROLL, real hands, real month-end work, a calendar showing three weeks passing]* *[ON-SCREEN CHAPTER CARD: "The report that came back to life"]*

CLAIM. *[ON-SCREEN CLAIM CARD]* Here's one we ran. A finance team had six dashboards and near-zero opens.

PROOF. *[MODALITY: SCREEN-RECORD, the before state, then the after]* For the first week we built nothing. We sat with two people while they did their actual month-end and watched where they went back to Excel. Every time it was the same two things. The dashboard's revenue didn't match their close file, and it took eleven seconds to load, so they had given up on it. We fixed exactly those two things. We reconciled the headline number to their close, down to the cent, and we cut the load from eleven seconds to under two by tidying the model underneath. We didn't add a chart. We removed nine of them and left the one view they used. This month against plan, top three gaps.

PAYOFF. *[MODALITY: DATA-VIZ, opens per week climbing from near zero]* And the change that got it opened again, the one I promised. We renamed the report to match the meeting it was for, put it one click from where they already worked, and had their own manager open it live in the Monday review. Within three weeks it was the first thing they checked every morning. Not from a new chart. From a number they trusted, a report that loaded fast, and a reason to open it that fit their week.

RE-HOOK. *[TALKING HEAD]* So let me put the whole method in one place, so you can run it yourself this week.

[9:15-10:15] PAYOFF — How do you fix this without breaking trust in the numbers? *[MODALITY: KINETIC TYPE, the five takeaways animate in one at a time as a numbered list]* *[ON-SCREEN CHAPTER CARD: "The five-step fix"]*

Here's the whole thing, and you can walk out with it even if you never talk to us.

  1. Name the one decision the report drives. No decision, no report.
  2. Reconcile the headline number to the source people already trust, down to the cent, before you ship.
  3. Keep the model, rebuild only the view, around that one decision.
  4. Version every change to a number, and tell people before they find it on their own.
  5. Put the report where people already work, and get their manager to open it first.

Do those five and the reason nobody uses your dashboards mostly goes away, because you removed every reason they had to close the tab.
