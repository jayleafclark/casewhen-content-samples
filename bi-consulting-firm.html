BI Consulting Firm vs. Big Consultancies: What's Actually Different

Quick answer

A bi consulting firm is a small specialist team that builds and fixes your Power BI, Fabric, and Azure reporting, then hands it back working. A big consultancy sells a large program staffed with layers of people. For most mid-sized companies, the specialist gets you a trusted number faster and for less money.

You are comparing a bi consulting firm against one of the big names because something in your reporting hurts. The month-end close drags into its second week. Two dashboards show two revenue numbers in the same board meeting. Your finance team exports everything back to Excel anyway. You want it fixed, and you want to know which kind of firm gets you there without a year of workshops.

The pain is common, and the numbers are ugly. 67% of organizations say they do not fully trust the data they use to make decisions (Precisely, 2025). Poor data quality costs the average organization $12.9M a year (Gartner). And 94% of business spreadsheets used in decisions contain errors (Poon et al., 2024). So the question is a fair one: who fixes this best for a company your size?

Most business intelligence consulting companies fall into one of two camps. There is the big generalist that does audit, strategy, tax, and a bit of data on the side. And there is the focused shop that does Microsoft BI and almost nothing else. They look similar in a proposal. They are very different to work with.

What does a bi consulting firm do differently?

A bi consulting firm sends the person who does the work to the call. When you ask a question about your semantic model or your refresh times, the answer comes from someone who has their hands on the report, not a manager reading a status slide.

The scope is narrow on purpose. A focused firm lives inside Power BI, Microsoft Fabric, and Azure all day. That means the fix for your revenue definition, your row-level security, or your slow refresh is a problem they solved last month for someone else. Hiring a consultant business intelligence teams will trust comes down to two things: seniority and focus. A specialist has both because that is the only thing they sell.

You also get a fixed, scoped piece of work. A dashboard build, a data model rebuild, a governance setup. You know what you are paying for and when it lands. The people who wrote the report are the people who train your team to own it.

Why do big consultancies cost more and move slower?

A big consultancy runs on a pyramid. A partner sells the work, a manager oversees it, and a rotating cast of junior staff builds it. You pay day rates across all of them, which is why a report that a specialist quotes at a fixed price becomes a six-figure program somewhere else.

The discovery phase is long because the team is learning your business from zero while billing for it. By the time they understand your close, months have passed. And when the engagement ends, the knowledge often walks out with them, so you call them back for the next change.

Plenty of firms bundle business intelligence and consulting into one glossy proposal, which makes the price hard to read. The line items sound impressive. What you are buying underneath is a lot of people and a long timeline.

What does good look like after six months?

Good is boring, and that is the goal. One agreed number that finance, sales, and the board all pull from the same place. A close you can finish in three days instead of eight. Dashboards people open instead of ignore.

That last one matters more than it sounds. Around 70% of dashboard work goes unused because the reports never earned trust (RevealBI). High performers close their books in under three days while the typical company sits at eight (SolveXia). And 82% of teams say data silos disrupt their day-to-day work (ZoomInfo). A good firm moves you toward those outcomes with something you can point at, not a strategy deck.

Trust is the whole game here. Only 40% of executives say they always trust their organization's data (Talend). Fixing that is a business result, and it shows up in faster decisions and shorter meetings, not in a longer report.

How do you pick between the two?

Start with the size of the problem. If you need your Power BI reports built or fixed and your numbers to reconcile, a bi consulting firm wins on speed, price, and the seniority of the people doing the work. If you are restructuring a whole global operating model across a dozen functions, a big consultancy has the bench for it.

Then ask three plain questions of anyone you talk to:

  • Who exactly will build the reports, and can I speak to them today?
  • What is the fixed price and the date I get a working result?
  • When you leave, does my team own it, or do I call you back?

When is a big consultancy the right call?

Sometimes it is the right call, and it helps to be straight about that. If your board wants a brand name on the invoice for governance reasons, a large firm buys you that cover. If the work spans strategy, change management, and reporting all at once, the bench depth is real. And if your company is large enough that a single specialist team cannot staff the scale, the pyramid exists for a reason.

For a mid-sized company that just needs trusted reporting, though, that scale is a cost you pay for capacity you will not use.

Frequently asked questions

### Is a bi consulting firm cheaper than a big consultancy?

Usually, yes, and the gap is wide. A specialist quotes a fixed scope with senior people doing the work, so you avoid paying day rates across a large team and a long discovery phase. The difference between a scoped project and a full program is often the difference between five figures and six.

### How long before we see results?

With a focused firm, weeks. A dashboard build or a data model fix ships in that window because the team already knows Power BI and Fabric inside out. A large program usually measures its first real result in months because the team learns your business as it goes.

### Do we lose the work when the consultants leave?

You should not, and this is a question worth asking upfront. A good bi consulting firm builds so your own team can run and change the reports, and trains them to do it. Ask any firm directly whether you own the work when they walk out the door.

### Can a small firm handle enterprise data?

Yes. Microsoft Fabric and Azure are built to scale, and a specialist that works with them daily handles large volumes as a matter of routine. Scale is about the platform and the design, not the size of the consultancy.

### What about Microsoft Fabric and Azure specifically?

This is where focus pays off most. A firm that does business intelligence and consulting across every tool cannot keep up with Fabric's release pace. A shop that lives in the Microsoft data platform every day can, which is why capacity sizing, cost control, and migrations tend to go smoother with a specialist.
