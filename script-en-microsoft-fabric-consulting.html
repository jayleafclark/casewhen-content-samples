[0:45-2:10] VALUE BEAT 1 - "What's Fabric, and how is it different from Power BI?" - custom animation CLAIM (on-screen text card): "Fabric is the platform. Power BI is one workload on it." "Quick grounding, because this trips up almost everyone. Power BI is the reporting tool you already know. Fabric is the bigger platform underneath it, and Power BI is one part of that. Fabric bundles seven workloads: data movement, data engineering, a data warehouse, data science, real-time data, databases, and Power BI reporting. All seven sit on one store called OneLake. The point of putting them together is one copy of your data that every part can read. OneLake stores it once, in an open format, and each workload reads the same files. That's the whole pitch: one store feeds many tools, and nothing gets copied between them." (MODALITY: custom animation.) [2:10-3:40] VALUE BEAT 2 - "Why would turning it on double my bill?" - data-viz CLAIM (on-screen text card): "Fabric bills you for a block of capacity by the hour." "Here's where the money changes. Power BI Pro is per user, around fourteen dollars a month each. Premium Per User is around twenty four. And an F64 on a one-year commitment lands around five thousand dollars a month. So the bill goes from a few per-user licenses to five thousand in one move. Same reports. Bigger invoice." [3:40-5:10] VALUE BEAT 3 - "How can my reports go offline when the metrics look fine?" - screen-record CLAIM (on-screen text card): "Fabric smooths your usage, then throttles you after the fact." "This is the one that catches teams out. Fabric doesn't bill each click the second it happens. It smooths your usage over time. A heavy report refresh gets spread out, so a big spike looks flat on the chart. That smoothing helps, right up until you're over your capacity for long enough. Then Fabric starts throttling. First it adds a delay to interactive actions. Then it starts turning requests away. Your reports go slow, then they stop responding, and the person watching the metrics sees usage that looks under control, because the spike got smoothed into the past." (MODALITY: screen-record of the Microsoft Fabric Capacity Metrics app, then a short custom animation cut inside the beat.) (SCREEN-RECORD: open the Fabric Capacity Metrics app. Cursor over the CU usage ribbon, then the carryforward / overage bar, then the throttling panel with its three stages: interactive delay, interactive rejection, background rejection.) (CUSTOM ANIMATION - capacity throttling, cut in mid-beat: a CU meter fills as request tiles arrive. One tall spike gets spread sideways into a flat line to show smoothing. A running total creeps up and crosses a red capacity line. Once over the line, new request tiles queue up, then get bounced back tagged "delayed," then "rejected.") (Visible change every 5-8s: alternate between the real metrics app and the animated meter so the abstract idea lands on the concrete screen.) PAYOFF (numbered takeaway on-screen): "Takeaway 3: If reports slow down for no clear reason, open the Fabric Capacity Metrics app and read throttling and carryforward, not just the live usage number. The live number can mislead you, because it's already been smoothed." RE-HOOK: "So Fabric costs more, and it needs watching. [5:10-6:40] VALUE BEAT 4 - "When is Fabric worth the capacity spend?" - talking head + b-roll CLAIM (on-screen text card): "Four signs Fabric is worth turning on." "Here are the four signs you're ready. One: you're already stitching together separate Azure services. A pipeline tool here, a warehouse there, a lake somewhere else, and a lot of copying between them. Fabric puts those on one store, so that's a real win for you. Two: you've got hundreds of people who only view reports. On per-user licensing every viewer costs money. At F64 those viewers are included, so past a couple hundred of them the capacity price starts to make sense. Three: you're on old Power BI Premium, the P-SKUs. Microsoft is retiring those and moving everyone to Fabric F-SKUs, so your hand is being forced anyway. Better to plan it than get surprised. Four: you want Copilot and AI features running on your own data, governed in one place. That lives in Fabric. One thing worth knowing though: speed here's a real dividing line. APQC found the top finance teams close their month in five days or fewer, while the slowest take ten or more. A single governed source of data is a big part of how the fast ones stay fast." [6:40-8:10] VALUE BEAT 5 - "When should I stay on Power BI Pro instead?" - data-viz CLAIM (on-screen text card): "Under about 200 users, per-user usually wins." "Now the stay-put list. If you've got a small group of report authors and a handful of viewers, per-user licensing is far cheaper. Let's do the rough math on screen. An F64 on a yearly commitment is about five thousand a month. Premium Per User is about twenty four dollars a month. Five thousand divided by twenty four is roughly two hundred and eight people. So under about two hundred users, per-user tends to win on price alone. Over that, capacity starts to pay off, especially with lots of view-only people. Second reason to stay put: you only need reports and dashboards, not the data engineering and warehouse pieces. If you're never going to touch the other six workloads, you're renting a platform to use one corner of it. Third: nobody on your team can watch capacity. Fabric needs someone reading that metrics app and right-sizing the SKU. No owner for that, and the bill drifts." (MODALITY: data-viz, break-even line chart.)
