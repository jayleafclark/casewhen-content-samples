(MODALITY: animation, a stream of request tickets flowing in from every department, all funnelling into one small team, the pile stacking up and the wait counter ticking upward) (ON-SCREEN: keyword card "DATA TEAM BOTTLENECK" lower-third, stays visible)

[0:12-0:45] PROMISE + STAKES + OPEN LOOP "By the end of this you'll understand why every report request queues through your data team, why that queue has almost nothing to do with how many analysts you hire, and what clears it for good. If you run data or operations, you already feel this. Sales wants a number, finance wants a number, the board wants a number, and all of it waits on the same few people. The average company runs more than a thousand apps, and about seventy percent of them don't talk to each other, and that's from a 2023 MuleSoft report. And near the end I'll show you the one move that lets your business people answer their own questions without you losing control of the numbers. Most teams get this exact move backwards." (MODALITY: talking head, one zoom-punch on "don't talk to each other") (ON-SCREEN: promise text "Why the queue forms → why headcount won't fix it → what clears it"; stat card "1,000+ apps per company, ~70% don't connect, MuleSoft, 2023")

[VALUE BEAT 1], 1:05, Chapter: "What is the queue really costing the business?" CLAIM: "The bottleneck doesn't just slow reports down. It slows down every decision that report was meant to feed." (MODALITY: b-roll, a boardroom where a slide is up and someone's shrugging, then a shot of an inbox full of "quick data request" emails) (ON-SCREEN: claim card "A slow report means a slow decision") PROOF: "Take a normal Monday pipeline review. The head of sales asks a simple question. Which deals slipped last week and why. The answer sits in a request that went to the data team on Thursday, and it's still in the queue. So the meeting runs on last month's export and a few gut calls. That's the cost. It isn't the hours your analysts spend, though those add up. It's the decisions made on stale numbers while the fresh ones wait. And remember those disconnected apps. Every question means stitching scattered systems together by hand, which is exactly why the answer sits in a queue. A queue behind the data team turns into hesitation at the top of the company." (MODALITY: on-screen text / kinetic type, the chain builds: "request → queue → stale meeting → slow decision") (ON-SCREEN: text "Scattered systems, stitched by hand, every time") PAYOFF: "Takeaway one. Measure the bottleneck by the decisions it delays, not the tickets in the backlog. That's the number your board cares about." (ON-SCREEN: numbered takeaway "1. Count delayed decisions, not delayed tickets") RE-HOOK: "So the obvious move is to throw people at it. More analysts, shorter queue. Here's why that almost never works."

[VALUE BEAT 2], 2:35, Chapter: "So do you just need to hire more analysts?" CLAIM: "Adding analysts shortens the queue for a few weeks, then it grows right back, because every new hire also becomes a person requests route through." (MODALITY: animation, the team grows from three people to six, the incoming request stream also thickens, and the pile settles back to the same height) (ON-SCREEN: claim card "More hands, same queue") PROOF: "Here's what happens on the ground. You hire two more analysts. Response times drop, everyone's relieved. Then word gets out that requests are getting answered, so more requests come in. Marketing wants their own view, ops wants a weekly cut, finance wants a variance report. Demand for numbers grows faster than any team you can hire. And every request still lands on a person who has to understand the ask, find the right data, write the query, and check it. You've added supply, and the demand grew to swallow it. This is why teams that double their data headcount often report the same backlog a quarter later. The queue isn't a staffing problem. It's a design problem, and you can't hire your way out of a design problem." (MODALITY: talking head, direct, one zoom-punch on "you can't hire your way out of a design problem") (ON-SCREEN: text "Demand for numbers grows faster than you can hire") PAYOFF: "Takeaway two. If your backlog returns every time you add people, stop treating it as a headcount gap. The shape of how work reaches your team is the thing to change." (ON-SCREEN: numbered takeaway "2. A returning backlog is a design signal, not a hiring signal") RE-HOOK: "Which raises the question everyone skips. If it's not people, what keeps rebuilding the queue every single week?"

[VALUE BEAT 3], 4:05, Chapter: "Then why does the queue keep coming back?" CLAIM: "The queue reforms because there's no trusted, shared layer between your raw data and your business people, so every request gets built from scratch." (MODALITY: animation, each incoming request drills all the way down to a messy pile of raw tables, and an analyst rebuilds the same joins and definitions over and over for every ticket) (ON-SCREEN: claim card "No shared layer = every request starts from raw") PROOF: "Walk through what your analyst does with a request. Someone asks for revenue by region this quarter. To answer it, the analyst goes to the raw tables, joins sales to customers to regions, decides which revenue definition to use, filters out the cancelled orders, and only then builds the chart. Now the next request comes in, gross margin by region, and a lot of that work starts over. Different person, slightly different definition of revenue, and now the two answers don't match. Multiply that by every department asking every week. Your team looks slow for one reason. They're rebuilding the same foundations for every single question, with no shared, trusted version they can all pull from. That missing middle layer is what forces everything through them." (MODALITY: b-roll, screen glimpse of two spreadsheets side by side showing two different revenue totals for the same quarter) (ON-SCREEN: text "Every answer rebuilt from raw → definitions drift → nobody can self-serve") PAYOFF: "Takeaway three. The queue exists because there's no governed layer people can safely build on. Fix the missing layer and most requests never need to reach your team." (ON-SCREEN: numbered takeaway "3. No governed layer means every request routes through you") RE-HOOK: "So let me show you what that layer is. This is the one part where I'll go into what your team does under the hood."

[VALUE BEAT 4], 5:35, Chapter: "What does governed self-serve mean for a team like yours?" CLAIM: "Governed self-serve means your team builds one certified model of the business, and everyone else asks their own questions against it without touching the raw data." (MODALITY: screen-record, Power BI Service, a workspace showing a certified semantic model with the endorsement badge, then a business user dragging fields into their own report from that model) (ON-SCREEN: claim card "One certified model, everyone builds on it") PROOF: "This is the one place I'll go a level deeper, because it's the move that changes everything. Your data team builds what's called a semantic model, once. Inside it, the tables are joined properly, revenue is defined one way, regions roll up correctly, and the whole thing refreshes on a schedule. Then your team certifies it, so it carries that endorsement badge in Power BI. And here's the part that clears the queue. A business user in finance opens that certified model and drags revenue and region onto a page themselves. No ticket. No waiting. This finance user is building their own report, and every number underneath comes from the model your team already vetted. Row-level security sits on top, so the sales manager for the north region only sees north rows, automatically, from the same model. Your team went from answering a hundred one-off requests to maintaining one trusted source that the whole company self-serves from. That's governed self-serve. Governed, because the definitions are locked by you. Self-serve, because people answer their own questions." (MODALITY: animation, the certified model sits in the centre, and finance, sales, and ops each pull their own reports directly from it, no line forming at the data team anymore) (ON-SCREEN: text "Certified semantic model + row-level security + self-serve reports") PAYOFF: "Takeaway four. Build one certified model your business people can safely self-serve from, and the queue stops forming, because the questions stop landing on your team." (ON-SCREEN: numbered takeaway "4. One certified model your business can self-serve from") RE-HOOK: "Now, if you've been burned before, you're already thinking this sounds like handing everyone the keys and watching it descend into chaos. Fair. Let me answer that head on."

[VALUE BEAT 5], 7:35, Chapter: "Won't letting people self-serve create chaos?" CLAIM: "Self-serve turns into chaos only when there's no governed model underneath. Governance is the thing that makes self-serve safe." (MODALITY: animation, split screen: on the left, ungoverned self-serve where everyone connects to raw data and the revenue numbers all drift apart; on the right, governed self-serve where everyone pulls from one certified model and the numbers agree) (ON-SCREEN: claim card "Governance is what makes self-serve safe") PROOF: "Most leaders tried self-serve once, handed everyone Power BI and access to the raw data, and got exactly what you'd expect. Fifty dashboards, five definitions of revenue, and more arguments than before. So they pulled it all back through the data team, and the queue returned. Here's the correction. In governed self-serve, people don't connect to raw data at all. They can only build on the certified model, which means they cannot invent their own version of revenue, because the definition lives in the model, set by your team. They get freedom over questions, and you keep control of definitions. Around seventy percent of dashboard work goes wasted because nobody trusts or uses the output, from RevealBI's figures. Governance is what flips that, because now every self-serve report is built on numbers people already agreed on. You're not choosing between control and speed. Governance is how you get both." (MODALITY: talking head, steady, one zoom-punch on "freedom over questions, control of definitions") (ON-SCREEN: stat card "~70% of dashboard work goes unused, RevealBI") PAYOFF: "Takeaway five. Don't hand out raw data. Hand out a governed model. That's the difference between self-serve that scales and self-serve that gets pulled back." (ON-SCREEN: numbered takeaway "5. Govern the model, then open up self-serve") RE-HOOK: "So think about your team six months after this lands. Here's what changes."

The data team bottleneck is a design problem, not a headcount problem. Requests queue because there's no governed, trusted layer between your raw data and your business people, so every question gets rebuilt from scratch and everything routes through the same few analysts. Adding staff shortens the queue for a few weeks, then demand for numbers grows to fill the new capacity. The way out is governed self-serve: your team builds one certified semantic model with agreed definitions and row-level security, and the rest of the company builds their own reports on top of it without touching raw data. That keeps definitions under your control while letting people answer their own questions, which is how the queue stops forming. Backed by figures from MuleSoft, RevealBI, and Gartner on disconnected apps, wasted dashboards, and the cost of poor data quality.
