Day 1 · MonENAustinazure data factory
Here is why your Azure Data Factory pipeline runs slow: ForEach is stuck in sequential mode.
ForEach has two settings that decide throughput: isSequential and batchCount. If isSequential is true, every iteration waits for the one before it, so ten tables copy one after another rather than together. Set isSequential to false and raise batchCount (up to 50) so the iterations run in parallel. Then look at the Copy activity itself. parallelCopies and Data Integration Units (DIU) are often left on the auto default, which caps how much each copy can move. Raise DIU and set parallelCopies to match the connection limit on your source, then watch the activity's throughput number in the monitoring view to confirm it climbed. A slow pipeline also delivers stale numbers to the morning report, and 67% of organizations already say they do not fully trust the data behind their decisions (Drexel LeBow and Precisely, 2025). On-time loads are part of earning that trust back.

Day 2 · TueENAustinazure synapse
There are three reasons an Azure Synapse dedicated SQL pool costs more than it should.
Reason one: the dedicated SQL pool stays online overnight and on weekends. Billing runs on Data Warehouse Units (DWU) for every hour the pool is provisioned, not per query, so an idle pool at 3am costs the same as one under full load. Set a schedule to take it offline when nobody's querying. Reason two: every workload routes through that one dedicated pool, including ad-hoc exploration and one-off checks. A serverless SQL pool prices per terabyte scanned, so light and occasional queries belong there, not on a provisioned pool you're paying for by the hour. Reason three: the DWU level gets set for the monthly peak and never moves. Scale up for the heavy load window, scale back down after it, and turn on result set caching so repeat queries don't re-scan the same data. All three are billing settings, and you can change every one of them this afternoon without rewriting a single query.

Day 3 · WedENAustinazure synapse analytics
Your Azure Synapse dedicated SQL pool bills 24/7 while queries run two hours a day.
Here is what I see in a lot of Azure Synapse Analytics setups. Someone provisions a dedicated SQL pool at DW200c or higher for a reporting workload, and the pool stays online every hour of every day. Dedicated pools bill on provisioned compute (DWU) whenever they are running, not on what you query. So you pay for a full day of capacity to serve a couple of hours of dashboard refreshes. Two fixes. First, stop the dedicated SQL pool when nobody is querying it. Taking the pool offline halts the compute meter, and you pay only for storage while it sits idle. You can schedule the stop and resume around your refresh window with a small automation runbook. Second, for exploratory or ad hoc queries, point people at a serverless SQL pool instead. Serverless bills per terabyte scanned, so an analyst poking at a few files costs cents rather than a reserved cluster. Before you scale the DWU up to make a slow report faster, check whether the pool is even offline overnight. Most of the bill comes from idle time rather than query time.

Day 6 · SatENAustinfabric capacity
If you think jumping to an F64 Fabric capacity will fix your slow reports, you're wrong.
Fabric capacity is a shared pool of Capacity Units, not a fixed allocation per report. When CU usage is high, Fabric smooths it across a 24-hour window and then throttles. First your interactive queries get an added delay, then background jobs like refreshes get rejected. A report can feel slow even though your F64 looks half-idle in the metrics app. The real problem is usually a few heavy items: a bloated semantic model, a DAX measure scanning the whole fact table, a refresh stacked on top of peak reporting hours. Bigger capacity gives you more CU headroom, but the same waste scales up with it. The fix that works: install the Fabric Capacity Metrics app, find the top CU-consuming operations, repair the model and the worst DAX, and move heavy refreshes off peak. Fabric passed 28,000 customers this year (Microsoft, Ignite 2025), and most of the slow tenants we see have a capacity-management problem rather than a size problem. Size the SKU to real usage after that, not before.

Day 10 · WedENAustinincremental refresh power bi
There are three things to get right before incremental refresh in Power BI works.
Most people flip the toggle on and wonder why the refresh still pulls the whole table. The toggle is the third step, and two things have to be in place before it does anything. One: create two datetime parameters named exactly RangeStart and RangeEnd. The names are case-sensitive, and the service looks for them by those names, so a typo means nothing happens. Two: filter your fact table on a real date column using those parameters, then confirm the step still query-folds. If the filter does not fold to the source, the source still hands over every row on each refresh and you gained nothing. Three: publish, then set the policy (archive X years, keep the last Y days). Now the service partitions the table and only refreshes the recent partitions. The payoff is time. Gartner puts the cost of poor data quality at $12.9M per year on average, and a chunk of that is reports nobody refreshed on schedule because a full reload ran for six hours. (Gartner, data-quality research.)

Day 29 · MonENAustinrow level security power bi
Nothing kills trust faster than a manager opening Power BI and seeing every region's salary numbers.
Here's how it usually happens. Someone builds a report with every region in one dataset, publishes it to a shared workspace, and assumes the workspace permissions keep people in their lane. They don't. Anyone with view access sees the whole thing. Row level security handles this in the data model itself, so what a person sees follows the data instead of the workspace. In Power BI Desktop you open Manage roles, create a role like 'Regional', and write a DAX filter on the table, for example [Region] = LOOKUPVALUE(UserRegion[Region], UserRegion[Email], USERPRINCIPALNAME()). That ties each signed-in user to their own rows. The step people skip is testing. Use View As in Desktop to load the report as a specific role or user before you publish. Two minutes there catches the gap that would otherwise surface in front of the whole leadership team. One caveat worth knowing: RLS does not apply to workspace admins or dataset owners, so test with a real member account, not your own login. This matters because 67% of organizations already say they don't fully trust the data behind their decisions (Drexel LeBow + Precisely, 2025). A number leaking into the wrong meeting only widens that gap.
How do you test RLS before a report goes wide, View As in Desktop or a separate member account in the Service?

Day 30 · TueDESajutableau to power bi migration
Warum dauert Ihre Migration von Tableau zu Power BI noch immer neun Monate?
Viele Teams behandeln die Migration als 1:1-Nachbau: Jedes Tableau-Dashboard wird einzeln in Power BI neu erstellt. Genau hier verstreichen die Monate. Der Aufwand entsteht nicht durch die Anzahl der Berichte, sondern durch doppelte Logik. In Tableau steckt die Berechnungslogik oft in jedem einzelnen Arbeitsblatt. Wenn Sie das direkt uebernehmen, kopieren Sie diese Logik in Dutzende getrennte Power-BI-Dateien. Sinnvoller ist ein gemeinsames semantisches Modell: Kennzahlen werden einmal in DAX definiert und von allen Berichten genutzt. Fuer grosse Faktentabellen richten Sie inkrementelles Aktualisieren mit RangeStart und RangeEnd ein, statt jede Nacht die volle Historie zu laden. Deployment-Pipelines trennen Entwicklung, Test und Produktion sauber voneinander. Der erste Schritt ist aber kein Neubau. Pruefen Sie zuerst die Nutzung, denn haeufig wird nur ein Teil der Tableau-Dashboards regelmaessig geoeffnet. Migrieren Sie genau diese und bauen Sie die Logik einmal zentral. Am Ende geht es um Vertrauen in die Zahlen: 67 Prozent der Organisationen vertrauen ihren Daten nicht vollstaendig (Drexel LeBow und Precisely, 2025). Eine Migration ist die Gelegenheit, das zu korrigieren. Kopieren Sie also nicht einfach die alten Berichte.
