Power BI Copilot: why it only works on clean data

Quick Answer: Power BI Copilot is the built-in AI assistant that writes DAX, builds report pages, and answers questions about your data in plain English. It only returns a trustworthy answer when there's one governed, well-modeled dataset behind it. Turn it on over messy data and it will give confident answers that are wrong. ---

You switched on Power BI Copilot, asked it for last month's revenue, and it handed back a number that doesn't match the one in your management report. Now you have an assistant that looks smart and gives you the wrong number, and no one is sure which figure to believe. This is the most common first experience teams have with copilot in Power BI, and it has almost nothing to do with the AI itself.

The reason is simple. Copilot answers questions using the data model you give it. If that model has three definitions of revenue and no clear owner, Copilot will pick one and state it with total confidence. The tool is only as reliable as the data underneath it. So before you plan a Copilot rollout, the real question for a Head of Data is whether your data is ready to be asked questions by anyone, human or machine.

What's Power BI Copilot and what can it actually do?

Power BI Copilot is Microsoft's AI assistant built into Power BI and Microsoft Fabric. You type a request in normal language and it does the work a report author would normally do by hand.

In practice it does four useful things:

  • Builds a report page for you from a prompt like "show sales by region for this year."
  • Writes and explains DAX measures, so you can ask "create a measure for year-over-year growth" instead of writing it yourself.
  • Summarizes a report in words, turning a dashboard into a short written readout.
  • Answers plain-language questions about the data through a chat pane, so a manager can ask "which product line dropped last quarter" without touching a filter.

None of that's magic. Copilot reads your semantic model, the tables, relationships, and measures, and works only from what's defined there. If a measure exists and is named clearly, Copilot uses it well. If it doesn't exist, or two versions exist, Copilot guesses, and that's where trust breaks.

Why does Power BI Copilot give different answers to the same question?

Ask two people to pull revenue and you often get two numbers. Copilot does the same thing, for the same reason.

Say your workspace has three datasets that each define revenue slightly differently. One includes tax, one nets out returns, one counts the invoice date instead of the ship date. A person knows which one to trust because they built it. Copilot doesn't. It reads whatever model the report is connected to and answers from that, so the same question against two reports returns two figures. Neither is a bug. They're two honest answers to a question your data model never settled.

This is why a Copilot rollout so often exposes a governance problem that was always there. The AI didn't create the conflicting numbers. It just asked them in front of everyone and made the gap impossible to ignore. When a wrong figure then flows into a forecast or an investor update, the cost of that unsettled model stops being theoretical.

Is your data ready for Power BI Copilot?

Here's the honest message most vendors skip. You can't just switch Copilot on and expect good answers. Your data has to be ready for it first, and for most companies it isn't yet.

Only 12% of organizations say their data is good enough, in both quality and access, to use for AI (Drexel LeBow and Precisely, 2025). That gap is the whole story. Copilot, and every other AI feature in Fabric, pays off on clean, governed, single-source data. Feed it the same tangle of exports and half-owned datasets your team works around today and it will produce answers that look polished and are wrong.

Being ready for Copilot is the same work as being ready to trust your reports at all:

  • One certified semantic model per subject, so there's a single place revenue is defined.
  • Clear measure names and descriptions, because Copilot reads them to understand what you mean.
  • One named owner per dataset, so someone is accountable when a number looks off.
  • Row-level security set correctly, so Copilot never shows a user rows they shouldn't see.

If you have those four, Copilot becomes useful. If you don't, it becomes a faster way to spread the wrong number.

What does a Power BI Copilot license cost, and what are the requirements?

Copilot isn't part of the standard Power BI Pro seat. The Power BI Copilot requirements include a paid capacity, so this is a budget line, not a free toggle.

To use it you need a paid Fabric or Power BI Premium capacity in your tenant. There's no standalone per-user Power BI Copilot license you can buy on its own. Once a qualifying capacity is running, Copilot is available to any Pro user assigned to it. That means the real decision is about capacity sizing and cost control, not about buying a per-seat add-on.

How do you get your data ready for Copilot?

You get ready for Copilot by doing the governance work you'd do anyway to trust your own reports. There's no Copilot-specific shortcut, and that's good news, because the work pays off with or without the AI.

Four steps get you most of the way:

  1. Pick one dataset per subject and mark it as a certified semantic model. This is the single source Copilot should read from. Turn off or hide the private copies people built on the side.
  2. Name and describe every measure in plain words. Copilot reads the measure name and description to decide what you mean, so "Revenue (net of returns)" works far better than "Rev2."
  3. Give each dataset one named owner and write it in the model settings. When Copilot returns something odd, you want a person to check it, not a shrug.
  4. Set row-level security and test it with View As before anyone uses Copilot, so the chat pane can never reveal rows a user isn't allowed to see.

Do these and Copilot stops being a risk and starts saving your team real hours. Skip them and no amount of AI will fix a data model that was never settled.

FAQ

Do I need Microsoft Fabric to use Power BI Copilot? You need a paid capacity, which is either a Fabric capacity or a Power BI Premium capacity. There's no separate per-user Copilot license. Once a qualifying capacity runs in your tenant, Pro users working in it can use Copilot.

Can Power BI Copilot make my numbers wrong? Copilot doesn't invent numbers, but it will confidently report a bad one if your data model has conflicting or undefined measures. It answers from whatever semantic model the report uses, so a messy model produces confident, wrong answers. Clean, certified models are what make its answers reliable.

Is Power BI Copilot worth turning on right now? It's worth it once you have one certified dataset, clear measure names, and row-level security in place. If your reports already disagree with each other, fix that first. Copilot rewards good data governance and punishes the lack of it.
