Power BI Premium Pricing Compared: What You're Actually Paying For

Quick answer. Power BI premium pricing has two real numbers. Premium Per User is $24 per person a month. Premium at the capacity level, now sold as a Microsoft Fabric F64, runs about $5,000 a month. Below both sits the $14 Pro seat. The tier you pick is a headcount question, not a guess. ---

Pick the wrong Power BI premium pricing tier and you either pay five figures a month for capacity that sits mostly idle or leave your report builders stuck on a seat that keeps hitting limits. Two numbers most buyers never see side by side run this decision. Premium Per User is $24 per person a month. Premium at the capacity level, now sold as a Microsoft Fabric F64, runs about $5,000 a month. Everything else is either the $14 Pro seat below it or the data underneath it, and the data is where most of the spend ends up. That is the whole map, and you can check most of it without a call.

You run the company or you sign for the spend. The quote in front of you shows a per-user price that looks small next to a capacity price that looks alarming, and no clear line telling you which one you need. So the decision stalls, or someone buys the big platform to be safe and you learn six months later that most of it sits idle.

What does Power BI premium pricing actually cover?

Two products carry the Premium name, and they are priced in completely different ways.

The first is Power BI Premium Per User, a $24 seat you assign to individual people. The second is Premium at the capacity level, a reserved block of compute you rent by the month regardless of how many people use it. Microsoft now sells that capacity as a Microsoft Fabric F-SKU, and the F64 is the size that matches what used to be called a P1. One is a per-person price. The other is a fixed monthly bill that covers a crowd.

Here is the ladder in plain numbers, so you can see where Premium sits against the seat below it.

TierWhat it costsWhat you getWho it's for
Power BI Pro$14 per user a monthPublish and open shared reportsPeople who only read dashboards
Power BI Premium Per User$24 per user a monthBigger data models, refreshes up to 48 times a day, paginated reportsThe handful of people who build and maintain reports
Premium capacity (Microsoft Fabric F64)About $5,000 a monthReserved compute so any number of viewers open reports without a per-seat licenseLarger rollouts with hundreds of viewers

Compared to the wider Power BI pricing picture, Premium is the tier you reach for when a few builders hit the ceiling on Pro, or when you have so many viewers that per-seat math stops making sense. Most companies need one of those two, rarely both at full scale.

Is Power BI Premium Per User worth $24 a month?

For the people who build reports, usually yes. For everyone else, no.

Premium Per User at $24 buys the report builders more room to work. Bigger models, refreshes that run up to 48 times a day instead of the 8 you get on Pro, and paginated reports for the pixel-perfect statements finance sends out. You put the five or ten people who actually build on the $24 seat and leave everyone who only opens a dashboard on the $14 Pro seat. People search the seat below this as Power BI pro pricing, one word, and it is the same $14 license either way.

The decision is a headcount question you can settle in one meeting. Count the builders. If that is a small group leaning on slow refreshes and models that keep hitting a limit, $24 each is cheap insurance. Count the readers. Keep them on Pro until the viewer number grows big enough that capacity becomes the cheaper road, which is the next question.

When does Premium capacity beat per-user seats?

Once you have a lot of viewers, the fixed capacity bill wins.

Premium capacity, the Fabric F64, costs about $5,000 a month on an annual reservation and lets any number of people open reports with no per-seat license for readers. So the break-even is a sum you can do on a napkin. Take your viewer count, multiply by $14, and compare it to $5,000. Below that line, Pro seats are cheaper. Above it, the capacity is cheaper and it stops you counting readers one by one. In round terms, somewhere past 300 to 400 report viewers the F64 tends to be the better buy.

You do not need to run this yourself. You need to know the number exists and ask whoever quotes you to show it at your real headcount. If they can't, that is your answer about the quote.

What happened to Premium P SKUs and Fabric?

Microsoft is retiring the old Premium capacity, the P SKUs, and moving that compute into Microsoft Fabric F SKUs. New Premium capacity now comes as Fabric, and existing P-SKU renewals are being wound down, with the migration deadlines landing across 2026 and 2028.

For a buyer, this matters for one reason. If your company already pays for a Premium P1 or larger, that line has an expiry on it, and the person who bought it may not have flagged the switch to Fabric. Before you renew anything with the word Premium on it, ask whether it is a P SKU on the way out, and what the Fabric equivalent costs on your usage. The Power BI fees you are quoted for a renewal should already assume Fabric pricing, not the old plan.

What makes the bill bigger than the license?

The data underneath the reports, not the tool on top. If sales and finance pull from different places with different definitions, no Premium tier makes them agree, and you have paid $24 a seat for a reporting layer people still keep a private spreadsheet next to.

The numbers back this up. Gartner puts the average cost of poor data quality at $12.9M a year per organization. And 67% of companies say they do not fully trust the data they use to make decisions (Drexel LeBow and Precisely, 2025). About 70% of the dashboards teams build go unused (RevealBI), usually because people don't believe the numbers. Every one of those is money spent on licenses and Azure bills that returns nothing, because the data going in was never sorted out.

You feel that spend somewhere other than the price sheet. A leadership review where sales quotes one revenue figure and finance quotes another, and twenty minutes go to arguing whose number is right. A month-end close that drags past a week because one person stitches the spreadsheets by hand, when high performers close in under three days (Solvexia). A decision made on last quarter's figure because nobody was sure the report was current. No Premium seat fixes any of that.

How do you keep Power BI premium pricing under control?

You keep it in check with four decisions, none of which need you to open a report.

  1. Split builders from readers. Put the few people who build on Premium Per User at $24, and everyone who only reads on Pro at $14. Don't buy the expensive seat for people who never touch the editor.
  2. Only move to capacity when the math says so. Multiply your viewer count by $14 and compare it to the $5,000 F64. Move to capacity when the seats cost more than the reservation, not before.
  3. Check what you already own before you renew. If you already pay for a Premium P SKU, confirm the Fabric migration timing and price before you sign another year at the old number.
  4. Give one person the numbers. One owner in finance or operations who protects a single definition per metric. It is the cheapest control you have, and the one most companies skip.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Power BI Premium Per User? It costs $24 per person each month. That buys bigger models, more frequent refreshes, and paginated reports on top of the $14 Pro seat. You assign the seat to the people who build reports and keep everyone else on Pro.

What is the difference between Power BI Pro and Premium Per User? Pro at $14 lets people publish and open shared reports. Premium Per User at $24 adds larger data models, refreshes up to 48 times a day, and paginated reports. Give the $24 seat to your report builders and leave the readers on Pro.

Is Power BI Premium capacity being replaced by Microsoft Fabric? Yes. The old Premium P SKUs are being retired and that capacity now comes as Fabric F SKUs, with the F64 matching the old P1. If you own a P SKU today, its renewal moves to Fabric pricing across 2026 and 2028, so check the timing before you sign.

When is Premium capacity cheaper than per-user seats? Once you have a large number of viewers. Multiply your viewer count by $14 and compare it to the roughly $5,000 monthly F64 capacity. Past about 300 to 400 viewers the fixed capacity tends to cost less and drops the per-reader seat count.

Does Power BI premium pricing include the cost of moving my data? No. Getting data into Power BI is a separate Azure line, billed by usage rather than per seat, and it keeps charging every month. When someone quotes a Premium project, ask for the licensing and the data plumbing as two separate numbers.
