Cognos to Power BI Migration: What a Head of Data Should Plan For

Quick Answer: A Cognos to Power BI migration means moving your reports, data models, and security rules off IBM Cognos and onto Microsoft Power BI. Do it in stages: rebuild the semantic model first, port the reports people actually use, match the security, then run both tools side by side until the numbers agree. Most teams finish the core work in three to six months.

You are staring at a Cognos to Power BI migration because your old reports still run the business, the license renewal keeps climbing, and fewer people left on the team know how to keep the whole thing alive. That is a hard spot to be in as a Head of Data. The reports work, so nobody wants to touch them, but every quarter the risk of one breaking gets bigger and the pool of people who understand the framework manager model gets smaller.

This guide walks through how to plan the move without breaking the numbers your board and your finance team rely on. It covers what to move first, how to keep every report trusted during the switch, what the cost and timeline really look like, and who inside your team should own the work.

Why are teams planning a Cognos to Power BI migration now?

Three things usually push the decision at the same time.

The first is money. Cognos licensing and the servers behind it cost more each year, and the bill rarely maps to how many people still open the reports. When you look at the usage logs, a handful of reports carry almost all the traffic and the rest sit untouched. You are paying for a full platform to keep a dozen reports running.

The second is people. The engineers who built your Cognos framework packages are retiring or moving on, and new hires arrive knowing Power BI, not Cognos. Every year the group that can safely change a report shrinks. When one report breaks the morning of a review, you want more than one person who can fix it.

The third is the pull toward the Microsoft stack. If your company already runs on Microsoft 365, Azure, and Excel, then Power BI sits inside tools your people open every day. Reports land in Teams, refresh from your Azure warehouse, and connect to Excel without a separate login. Microsoft has led Gartner's Analytics and BI Magic Quadrant for 17 years running (Gartner, 2024), so you are moving toward a platform that will still be supported and hired for a decade from now.

None of this means Cognos was a bad choice when you bought it. It means the ground under it has shifted, and a planned move beats a rushed one after a report breaks.

What should you move first when you migrate from Cognos to Power BI?

The biggest mistake in a Cognos report migration is treating it as a copy job, where every report gets rebuilt one for one in the new tool. You do not want that. You want to move what earns its place and drop the rest.

Start with an inventory. Pull the Cognos usage logs for the last twelve months and rank every report by how often it is actually opened and by whom. You will almost always find that a small set of reports drives most of the value, a middle group runs occasionally, and a long tail has not been touched in a year. Only the first two groups need to move.

Then rebuild in this order:

  1. The data model first, not the reports. Cognos hides its business logic inside framework manager packages. Power BI puts that logic in a semantic model built on a star schema. Get the model right first, with clean tables, clear relationships, and the measures defined once, and every report you build on top of it will return the same number. Skip this step and you rebuild the old mess in a new tool.
  2. The reports people depend on. Port the daily and monthly reports that finance, sales, and operations open by name. Rebuild them as proper Power BI reports, not screenshots of the old layout. This is your chance to fix the parts everyone worked around by hand.
  3. Row-level security. Cognos controls who sees what through its own security model. In Power BI you set this with row-level security rules tied to the user. Match the old access exactly, because the fastest way to lose trust in a new tool is to let someone see a number they should not.
  4. The occasional reports, last. The middle group moves once the core is stable and proven.

The long tail does not move at all. Archive it, tell the owners, and give anyone who objects a two-week window to make the case. Most never will.

How do you keep the numbers trusted during the switch?

This is the part that decides whether the migration is called a success or a mess. If a report shows one revenue figure in Cognos and a different figure in Power BI on the same day, people stop trusting both, and your whole project loses its footing.

Run the two systems in parallel and reconcile them. For every report you move, produce the same numbers in Cognos and in Power BI for the same period and line them up cell by cell. When they match for a full close cycle, the Power BI version is trusted and the Cognos one can retire. When they do not match, you have found a real difference in logic, and it is far better to find it now than after finance has filed on the new number.

Write down the reason behind every rule as you rebuild it. Cognos models carry years of small decisions about how a metric is calculated, which rows to exclude, how the fiscal calendar works. Much of that lives only in the framework, not in any document. As you port each measure, capture the why, so the number is defensible when someone asks where it came from.

Keep one person accountable for sign-off on each report. Not a committee. One named owner who confirms the new report matches and agrees to switch off the old one. That single decision point is what keeps a migration from stalling half-finished with both tools running at once and the bill doubled.

Cognos vs Power BI: what actually changes for your team?

The daily experience is different in ways worth setting expectations for. Here is a plain comparison of what your team moves from and to.

The Cognos vs Power BI difference your team will feel first is the talent pool. Report changes that once waited on one Cognos specialist can be handled by more people, which is the point of the move.

What does a Cognos to Power BI migration cost and how long does it take?

The honest answer is that it depends on how many reports you truly keep, not on how many you have today. A team that keeps thirty core reports moves far faster than one that insists on porting three hundred.

For a mid-sized team, the core migration usually runs three to six months from inventory to the first reports going live and trusted. The model and the top reports come first, then the reconciliation cycle, then the occasional reports. The long tail is archived, not rebuilt, which is where teams save the most time.

Cost has two parts. The one-time build is the model, the reports, the security, and the reconciliation work. The ongoing cost is Power BI licensing, which scales with how many people use it rather than sitting as a large fixed server bill. For most teams the ongoing number lands lower than the Cognos renewal it replaces, but that gap only shows up after you retire the old servers, so budget to run both for a few months during the overlap.

The cost that surprises people is running both tools longer than planned because the reconciliation never finishes. A firm timeline for switching off Cognos, report by report, is what keeps that from happening.

Who should own a Cognos to Power BI migration inside your team?

Give the migration one owner with the authority to retire the old reports. That person does not have to write every measure. They do need to decide when a Power BI report is trusted enough to replace its Cognos original, and they need to keep the archive list firm when someone lobbies to bring a dead report back.

Under that owner, you want a small build team that knows the Power BI semantic model well, because the model is where the migration is won or lost. Getting the star schema and the measures right once means every report agrees. Getting it wrong means you rebuild the old confusion with a new logo on it.

Many teams bring in outside help for the model and the reconciliation, then keep the reports and future changes in-house. That keeps the trickiest, most permanent decisions in expert hands while your own people build the skills to run it afterward.

FAQ

How long does a Cognos to Power BI migration take? For a mid-sized team, the core work usually takes three to six months from inventory to the first trusted reports going live. The timeline depends far more on how many reports you keep than on how many you have, because the long tail gets archived rather than rebuilt.

Can you migrate Cognos reports automatically to Power BI? There are tools that convert some Cognos objects, but a clean cognos report migration is a rebuild, not a straight copy. The value is in fixing the data model and dropping unused reports, which an automated one-for-one conversion cannot do for you.

Will the numbers match after we migrate from Cognos to Power BI? They will if you run both tools in parallel and reconcile each report cell by cell for a full close cycle before retiring the Cognos version. Where they do not match, you have found a real difference in the old logic, which is exactly what the reconciliation step is meant to catch.
