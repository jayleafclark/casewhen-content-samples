SPOKEN: "OneLake is the part that makes the whole thing click. It's a single storage layer under every workload in your Fabric tenant. Your pipeline lands the data once. Then the warehouse, the notebook, and Power BI all read that same copy. No exporting a table out of one tool and importing it into the next. No four versions of revenue drifting apart in four places. And when the data already lives somewhere else, in Azure storage or even Amazon S3, Fabric can point at it with a shortcut. The workload reads it in place, with nothing copied and nothing duplicated. This is part of why only about twelve percent of teams say their data is in good enough shape to feed AI. Most teams have the same numbers scattered across a dozen stores. One copy underneath everything is how that stops."

PAYOFF (ON-SCREEN numbered card): "Takeaway 3: OneLake gives every workload one shared copy of the data, so Power BI reports off the same numbers your pipelines produce."

[6:25-7:50] VALUE BEAT 5, What does switching Fabric on cost me? (MODALITY: data-viz) (ON-SCREEN claim card: "Fabric is billed as a capacity you rent by the hour, not per user.")

CLAIM: Fabric is billed as a capacity you rent by the hour, not per user.

PROOF (DATA-VIZ + a short CUSTOM ANIMATION of throttling): First a side-by-side card. Left "Power BI Pro = per user": ten little seat icons, each with a small price tag, total scales with headcount. Right "Fabric = per capacity (F SKU)": one compute pool billed by the hour, a slider from F2 up to F64, with a callout on F64 reading "viewers stop needing their own Pro license". Then the throttling animation: a CU meter (capacity units) that fills as requests come in. Usage spikes above the line; a "smoothing" band spreads the spike over time. Push the spikes higher and longer, the meter pins at the top, and new requests visibly queue up behind a gate labelled "throttling", while a second gauge labelled "capacity metrics" still reads a middling green. Caption: "looks fine on the dashboard, slow in the report".

SPOKEN: "This is where the money changes shape. Power BI Pro is a per person price. Ten seats, ten licenses, done. A Fabric capacity works differently. You rent a pool of compute, an F SKU, billed by the hour whether you're using it or not. The smallest is an F2. The one people care about is F64, because at F64 and above your report viewers don't each need a Pro license, which is why bigger orgs jump to it. But renting compute has a twist most teams meet the hard way. Fabric smooths your usage over time to handle spikes. Push past what your capacity can carry for long enough and it starts throttling. Reports slow down, refreshes queue, and the capacity metrics can still look fine while it's happening. So Fabric costs you in two ways. The hourly sticker for the capacity, and the effort of sizing it right and watching it, or you pay for a platform that slows down under load."

PAYOFF (ON-SCREEN numbered card): "Takeaway 5: Power BI Pro scales by seats. Fabric scales by capacity, and an undersized capacity throttles under load with no obvious cause."

(RE-HOOK) "Alright. You know what each one is and what it costs. So what do you do with this on Monday morning?"

SPOKEN: "Here's the ten minute version you can run yourself. Question one. Is anyone on your team building or maintaining data pipelines, or does the data already arrive clean from somewhere else? If it arrives clean and you only report on it, mark that as a point for staying on Pro. Question two. Do you need a lakehouse, a warehouse, or real time data in the same place as your reports? If yes, that's a point for Fabric. Question three. How many people only view reports versus build them? A large viewer audience is the thing that tips the math toward an F64 capacity, because those viewers stop needing their own licenses. Add it up. Reporting only, small viewer count, you stay on Power BI Pro and you've just saved yourself a capacity bill. Pipelines, a lakehouse, and hundreds of viewers, you scope a Fabric capacity and you size it against your real usage instead of a guess. That's the whole decision, and you got to the shortlist without a consultant."

It's your viewer-to-author ratio. A team of ten authors and no viewers almost never needs Fabric for cost reasons. A handful of authors feeding reports to five hundred viewers is exactly where an F64 capacity starts saving money instead of adding it."
