Power BI license cost in 2026: what you pay per user, and why

Quick answer. Your Power BI license cost in 2026 has three tiers: Power BI Desktop is free, Power BI Pro is $14 per user a month, and Premium Per User is $24 per user a month. Larger teams buy Fabric capacity instead, where an F64 reservation runs about $5,000 a month and lets viewers read reports without a Pro seat. ---

Most teams get their Power BI license cost wrong, and it shows up as a monthly bill for Pro seats that half the company never opens. The pricing looks simple on the Microsoft page. The trouble starts when you match the wrong tier to the wrong person, or when you move to Fabric capacity a year too early. Below is the plain math, tier by tier, with the 2026 numbers you can check against a quote.

What does a Power BI license cost in 2026?

Microsoft sells Power BI in three buckets, and your Power BI license cost depends on which one each person needs.

  • Power BI Desktop is the free app you build reports in. Power BI Desktop cost is zero on any Windows machine.
  • Power BI Pro costs $14 per user each month. Anyone who publishes reports, or opens a report shared in a normal workspace, needs a Pro seat.
  • Power BI Premium Per User is $24 per user a month. It adds larger data models, more frequent refreshes, and paginated reports for one price per person.

Microsoft raised these prices in April 2025, the first increase since Power BI launched. Pro went from $10 to $14, and Premium Per User went from $20 to $24. So if you budgeted off an old quote, your real bill is about 40% higher today.

Why is Power BI Pro priced per user, and what do you get?

Power BI Pro is priced per user because Microsoft charges for the right to share and read content, not for the desktop app. Building a report is free. The moment you want other people to open it, someone pays.

A Pro seat gives one person the right to publish to a workspace, share dashboards, and use deployment pipelines to move a report from a test workspace to production without rebuilding it by hand. Pro also caps you at a 1 GB data model and up to 8 scheduled refreshes a day. For a finance team pulling one nightly load, that is plenty. For a sales team that wants hourly numbers on a two-year history, you will feel those limits fast.

The trap most buyers walk into: they buy a Pro seat for every name in the directory, including people who only glance at one dashboard once a month. At $14 each, 200 casual viewers is $33,600 a year for seats that sit idle most of the time.

What happened to the Power BI Premium price under Fabric?

The old Power BI Premium price used to be a per-capacity plan called P1, P2, and P3. Microsoft retired those and folded the capacity into Microsoft Fabric, so new buyers now purchase F-SKUs instead.

Fabric capacity is sold by the hour and reserved by the month. An F2, the smallest, runs around $263 a month pay-as-you-go. The one number that matters for most buyers is F64. At an F64 reservation, about $5,000 a month, your viewers can read published reports without each one needing a Pro seat. That single rule is why the Power BI Premium price question almost always turns into a head-count question.

Premium and Fabric capacity also let you set up incremental refresh. Instead of reloading a large table from scratch every night, you define RangeStart and RangeEnd parameters and the load only touches the newest rows. A ten-year sales table that took 40 minutes to refresh can drop to under a minute once the old partitions stop reloading. That saved refresh time is part of what you pay for at the higher tiers.

What about Power BI Desktop cost and the certification exam?

People mix two very different numbers together here, so let me split them.

Power BI Desktop cost is zero. You download the app, build your data model, write your DAX measures, and publish, all without paying Microsoft a cent for the tool itself. The bill only starts when you share.

The Power BI certification cost is a separate thing entirely. The PL-300 exam, officially the Microsoft Power BI Data Analyst Associate, runs about $165 in the United States and a bit less in some countries. It proves a person knows the platform. It is a personal credential, and it changes nothing about what your reports cost to run. One or two people with a PL-300 is enough for most small teams.

Which license does your team really need?

Spending more on licenses rarely fixes a reporting problem. In one 2025 survey, 67% of organizations said they do not fully trust the data they use to make decisions (Drexel LeBow and Precisely, 2025). A bigger license does not change that number. Cleaner data models and clear ownership do, and they cost far less than an extra tier of seats.

Here is the order we use with clients before anyone buys a thing:

  1. Count builders versus viewers. Every person who publishes or edits a report needs Pro. Pure viewers might not, if you go to capacity.
  2. Add up the Pro seats. Multiply your viewer count by $14. If you are paying for more than about 350 Pro viewers, run that total against an F64 reservation at roughly $5,000 a month. Near that head count, capacity gets cheaper per person.
  3. Test security before you widen access. Use View As in Power BI Desktop to check your row-level security roles, so you never pay for seats on a report that shows the wrong customer the wrong numbers.
  4. Turn on incremental refresh early. It keeps a Pro workspace under the 1 GB model limit, which stops you from jumping to Premium Per User before you truly need it.
  5. Budget the certification apart from licenses. The PL-300 fee is a one-time training cost, not a running charge.

The break-even math is worth doing by hand. A $5,000 F64 reservation divided by $14 a seat is about 357 viewers. Below that, per-user Pro is usually cheaper. Above it, capacity wins, and every extra viewer past that line is basically free. Most teams overspend because they never run that one division.

Frequently asked questions

### How much does a Power BI Pro license cost per user?

$14 per user a month as of 2026, up from $10 before the April 2025 increase. It covers publishing, sharing, and reading reports in a standard workspace.

### Is Power BI Desktop really free?

Yes. Power BI Desktop cost is zero to download and build reports on Windows. You only pay once you publish and share through Pro or a Fabric capacity.

### What is the Power BI Premium price now?

Premium moved into Fabric capacity. The Power BI Premium price is now a monthly capacity fee, starting around $263 for an F2 and about $5,000 for a reserved F64.

### How much is the Power BI certification cost?

The PL-300 exam is about $165 in the United States. The Power BI certification cost is a personal exam fee, separate from any product license your reports need.

### When does Fabric capacity beat per-user Pro?

Roughly past 350 viewers. At $14 a seat, 357 Pro viewers cost about the same as a $5,000 F64 reservation, and every viewer above that is cheaper on capacity.

### Do report viewers need a paid license?

In a Pro workspace, yes, every viewer needs a $14 Pro seat. On an F64 capacity or higher, free users can read published reports without one.
