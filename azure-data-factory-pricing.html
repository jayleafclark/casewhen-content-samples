Azure Data Factory Pricing, Line by Line, With Real Numbers

Quick answer. Azure Data Factory pricing is usage based, not per seat. You pay about a dollar per 1,000 pipeline activity runs, plus hourly compute for moving data and a small monthly charge per pipeline. For a mid-sized company on a daily schedule, that lands in the tens to low hundreds of dollars a month. ---

You are asked to sign off on a data project, and the one cost you cannot pin down is the Azure Data Factory pricing, because that line carries no fixed number, only a meter that bills you every month. There is no per-seat license here. You pay for how much data you move, how often you move it, and how many pipelines you keep running. That is the whole shape of it, and most of it you can sanity-check yourself before anyone quotes you a project.

You run the company. You sign off on the spend. Someone hands you a data project quote with a Power BI seat number that looks small and an Azure line that has no fixed price at all, just a meter. So the decision stalls, or you approve it and then watch the Azure bill drift every month with no clear reason why.

The cost you feel sits away from the tool. It shows up in the leadership review where sales quotes one revenue figure and finance quotes another, and twenty minutes go to arguing about whose number is right. It shows up in a month-end close that drags past a week because the data arrives late or arrives wrong. Azure Data Factory is the part that collects your data, cleans it, and loads it somewhere your reports can read. When it is set up well, the numbers show up on time and match. When it is not, you pay for the compute anyway and still get late, mismatched reports.

What does azure data factory pricing include?

Azure Data Factory bills you in a few separate ways, and none of them is a flat monthly subscription. Here is the meter without the sales deck.

Cost lineHow Microsoft bills itRough numberWhat drives it up
Pipeline activity runsPer 1,000 runsAbout $1 per 1,000 runsHow many steps run, and how often
Data movement (copy)Per compute-hour while it runsCents to a few dollars an hourVolume of data you move
Data flows (transforms)Per compute-hour while they runA few dollars an hourHeavy cleaning and reshaping
Active pipelinesSmall flat monthly feeUnder a dollar per pipeline a monthHow many pipelines you keep

Two things catch buyers off guard. First, the meter never stops on its own. An active pipeline charges a small amount every month whether it does much work or not, so old pipelines nobody uses still show up on the bill. Second, the compute is the swing factor. Moving a gigabyte on a nightly schedule costs almost nothing. Moving a lot of data every hour, with heavy cleaning on the way, is where the number climbs.

How much will azure data factory pricing cost you each month?

For a normal mid-sized company loading its sales, finance, and operations data once a day, azure data factory pricing tends to land somewhere in the tens to low hundreds of dollars a month. A few thousand activity runs a day is a couple of dollars. The nightly compute to move and clean the data is usually the biggest slice, and even that is modest on a daily cadence.

The bill grows in two directions you can predict. More data moved per run pushes the compute hours up. More frequent runs, say every hour instead of once a night, multiply the whole thing. So before anyone commits you to real-time everything, ask a plain question: does the business need the number refreshed every hour, or is once a day fine? That single answer moves the Azure bill more than any tuning a consultant can do later.

What does Power BI pro cost on top of that?

Azure Data Factory is the plumbing. Power BI is what your people actually open to read the numbers, and it has its own price. The Power BI pro price is $14 per user a month, and that is the seat almost every team lands on, because the free tier does not let people share reports with each other.

The cost of Power BI pro is easy to size. Take the number of people who open dashboards and multiply by $14. For 40 people that is about $560 a month, which is a rounding error next to payroll. Power BI Pro costs the same whether those people look once a week or all day, so the seat count is the only lever. There is a heavier tier, Premium Per User at $24 a month, but you only put that on the handful of people who build reports, not on everyone who reads them.

Put the two bills side by side and the picture is clear. The Power BI seats are a small, fixed, easy number. The Azure Data Factory line is the one that moves, and it is the one worth watching.

Why is the data bill bigger than the license?

The expensive part is almost never the tool. It is the data underneath and what happens when it is wrong. If sales and finance pull from different places with different definitions, no amount of Azure compute or Power BI seats makes them agree, and you end up paying for a reporting setup people still do not trust.

The numbers back this up. Gartner puts the average cost of poor data quality at $12.9M a year per organization. About 67% of companies say they do not fully trust the data they use to make decisions (Drexel LeBow and Precisely, 2025). And roughly 70% of the dashboards teams build go unused (RevealBI), usually because people keep a private spreadsheet on the side. High performers close their books in under three days, while a week or more is common for everyone else (Solvexia). Every one of those is real money that a bigger Azure meter does not fix.

So the spend that matters sits well past the $1 per 1,000 runs and the $14 seat. The weight lands in the wasted licenses, the slow close, and the meeting where two right-looking numbers disagree and a decision gets made on the wrong one.

How do you keep the whole cost under control?

You keep it in check with four decisions, and not one of them needs you to open a report.

  1. Ask for both bills as separate lines. The Power BI seats are one number and the Azure Data Factory meter is another. If a quote blends them, ask for them split, because the seat cost is fixed and the Azure cost is the one that drifts.
  2. Match the schedule to the need. Refreshing every hour when once a day would do can multiply the Azure bill for no business gain. Decide the cadence at the business level, then let the build follow it.
  3. Split builders from viewers on Power BI. Keep the people who only read on the $14 Pro seat and put the few who build on the heavier tier. Do not buy the expensive seat for people who never open the editor.
  4. Give one person the numbers. One owner in finance or operations who protects a single definition per metric. This is the cheapest control you have, and it is the one most companies skip.

Here is the one part that goes a level deeper, kept short. The Azure Data Factory bill is mostly a function of two dials: how much data each run moves, and how often runs fire. Cut either one and the compute cost drops with it. A lot of companies pay for hourly refreshes on data the business only looks at each morning. You do not need to tune this yourself. You need to know the two dials exist and ask whoever is quoting you to show you the monthly number at a daily cadence versus an hourly one.

Frequently asked questions

How is Azure Data Factory priced? Azure Data Factory pricing is usage based, not per seat. You pay about a dollar per 1,000 pipeline activity runs, hourly compute while data is moving or being cleaned, and a small monthly charge for each active pipeline. There is no fixed subscription, so the bill tracks how much data you move and how often.

How much does Azure Data Factory cost per month for a mid-sized company? For a company loading its data once a day, it usually runs from the tens to the low hundreds of dollars a month. The nightly compute to move and clean the data is normally the largest slice. Running things every hour instead of once a night is what pushes the number higher.

What is the difference between Azure Data Factory pricing and Power BI pricing? They are two separate bills. Azure Data Factory is usage based and charges for moving and preparing data. Power BI charges per person, and the Power BI pro price is $14 per user a month. Ask for both as separate lines on any quote.

How much does Power BI Pro cost? The cost of Power BI pro is $14 per user a month, which lets people publish and open shared reports. The free tier is $0 but does not allow sharing between people, so any real team lands on Pro. Power BI Pro costs the same regardless of how heavily each person uses it.

Why does my Azure Data Factory bill keep changing? Because it is a meter, not a subscription. The two things that move it are how much data each run moves and how often the runs fire. Old pipelines nobody uses also keep charging a small monthly fee, so a bill that drifts up often means more frequent refreshes or pipelines left running.
