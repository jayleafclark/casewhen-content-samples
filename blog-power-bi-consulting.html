Power BI Consulting: How to Tell a Good Firm From a Bad One

Quick answer. Power BI consulting is paid, hands-on help to design your data model, build reports people trust, and set up refresh, security, and governance so the numbers stay correct at month-end. Good Power BI consulting proves its work on a live dataset, not slides, and leaves your own team able to run the build after it's done. ---

67% of organizations don't fully trust the data they use to make decisions (Drexel LeBow and Precisely, 2025), and weak Power BI consulting is a big reason that number stays high. The dashboard looks great in the demo. A month later, the board pack shows one revenue figure, the finance export shows another, and the person who built it has moved on. Most teams pick a firm on price and portfolio, then find out the expensive part was everything they couldn't see in a slide deck.

This is a plain guide to evaluating Power BI consulting the way we wish more buyers would: on the model, the refresh, the security, and the handover, not the colors. If you've got a shortlist, or you're writing the brief, read this before you sign anything. It'll save you a rebuild six months from now.

Why does your Power BI project stall after the first dashboard?

Here's the usual sequence. A consultant builds a good-looking report on a live connection, ships it, and leaves. The data model underneath is thin: no clean star schema, measures typed straight into visuals, one .pbix file living on the builder's laptop. It all runs fine at demo size.

At real size, the scheduled refresh starts timing out, because a full reload of a 200-million-row fact table can exceed the 1 GB model limit on a Power BI Pro workspace. Now someone has to rerun it by hand every morning, and people stop trusting the numbers. Gartner puts the cost of poor data quality at $12.9M per year for the average organization, and a chunk of that is teams re-checking reports they've stopped believing.

None of that shows up in a demo. You see it at the first month-end, when the refresh stalls at 8am and the board meeting is at 9. Good Power BI consulting designs for that morning on day one.

How do you evaluate a Power BI consulting firm before you sign?

Give the shortlist a small, real problem from your own data and watch how they reason. Five questions tell you most of what you need, and you're listening for specific answers, not reassurance.

  1. Ask to see a semantic model they built. A strong BI consulting firm will show you relationships, a proper date table, and named DAX measures. If the answer stays vague here, expect a thin build.
  2. Ask how refresh works at your volume. You want to hear "incremental refresh with RangeStart and RangeEnd" or "Direct Lake on Fabric," with a number attached, not "it refreshes every night."
  3. Ask how they handle who-sees-what. The answer should name row-level security and the View As role tester in Power BI Desktop, which proves a regional manager sees only their rows before the report ships.
  4. Ask how changes get promoted. Good firms use Power BI deployment pipelines with separate Development, Test, and Production workspaces, so a bad change gets caught before the people running the business ever see it.
  5. Ask what you keep if they walk away tomorrow. The .pbix files, the documentation, and workspace access all belong to you. If the model lives only on their machine, you're renting your own reports.

A firm that answers these plainly has done this before. One that gets defensive is telling you something too.

What does good business intelligence consulting actually build?

A finished build has a shape you can check against. Business intelligence consulting done well leaves you with a short, boring list of things you can rely on:

  • A star schema: fact tables in the middle, dimension tables around them, and one shared date table. It's simple to filter and fast to query.
  • One definition per metric. "Revenue" is a single DAX measure in a certified semantic model, so every report that touches it agrees to the cent.
  • A refresh you can rely on: scheduled refresh, incremental refresh on the big tables, and an on-premises data gateway if the source sits behind your firewall.
  • Row-level security tested with View As, so access is proven before launch, not assumed after a complaint.
  • A usage metrics report, so you can see which reports people actually open and which ones to retire.
  • Written documentation and a training session, so your analyst can change a measure without opening a support ticket.

If the team works in Microsoft Fabric, the data can sit once in OneLake and be read by Power BI, notebooks, and warehouses without anyone copying it around. That's worth doing when several teams need the same tables. It's overkill for a five-person finance shop, and an honest firm will tell you which one you are.

Licensing sits underneath all of these and is separate from the fee. Power BI Pro runs about $14 per user per month, Premium Per User about $24, and a Fabric capacity is billed by compute (an F-SKU), not per person. A firm that skips licensing in the first call will surprise you on the invoice.

The cost that matters most is what you own at the end. A cheaper build that only its author understands gets expensive the day that person is unreachable. Less than half of data and analytics teams effectively deliver value to their organization (Gartner, 2023), and knowledge walking out the door is a big part of why.

How do you keep control after the consulting firm leaves?

The goal of good Power BI consulting is to make itself unnecessary. Three habits get you there.

First, insist on documentation as a deliverable, not a favor. That means the data model, the measures, the refresh schedule, and the security roles, all written down in a place your team can find.

Second, keep the build somewhere you control: the workspace in your own tenant, the .pbix files in your own storage, and measures your analyst can read and edit. A model that only opens on the consultant's laptop is a bill waiting to happen.

Third, book a training session before the final invoice, so at least one person in-house can open the semantic model and change a measure without calling anyone.

94% of business spreadsheets used in decision-making contain errors (Poon et al., 2024). The whole point of paying for Power BI consulting is to move off that and onto numbers you can defend in a board meeting. You only get that lasting result if the trusted model stays yours after the firm packs up.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between Power BI consulting and business intelligence consulting? They overlap heavily. Business intelligence consulting is the broader label and can cover any reporting tool. Power BI consulting means the work is built specifically in Power BI and Microsoft Fabric. If your company is standardized on Microsoft, you want someone who lives in that stack, not a generalist learning it on your budget.

Do I need Power BI consulting if I already own the licenses? Owning the licenses is like owning a word processor. The tool is there; the reliable model, the trusted measures, and the tested security are the real work. Most teams bring in help for the first solid build and the handover, then run it themselves from there.

How long does a first Power BI consulting project take? A focused first dashboard with a clean model, incremental refresh, and row-level security usually runs four to eight weeks. Anyone promising a trustworthy company-wide reporting build in a week is promising a demo, not something you can rely on at month-end.

Should I hire a big consultancy or a small BI consulting firm? Big firms give you process and bench depth. An independent BI consulting firm usually puts the senior person on your actual project instead of a junior doing the build. For a first Power BI or Fabric project, senior hands-on time tends to matter more than the logo on the invoice.

What's the biggest mistake buyers make with Power BI consulting? Approving on the demo and skipping the handover conversation. Ask how you'll run the reports after the firm leaves, and ask it on day one, in writing.
