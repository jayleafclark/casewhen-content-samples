Microsoft Fabric Consulting: A Straight Answer on What's Actually New

Quick answer. Microsoft Fabric consulting helps you decide whether Microsoft Fabric is worth turning on, then sets it up so your numbers stay trustworthy and your bill stays predictable. What's new is real: OneLake as one shared store, and Direct Lake for faster reports. Most companies need clean data first, not Fabric on day one. ---

Microsoft Fabric consulting is help with one decision: whether to turn on Microsoft Fabric at all, and if you do, how to set it up so it pays off instead of running up a bill you never planned for. Here's the number that makes this timely. Fabric passed 28,000 customers in about two years (Microsoft, Ignite 2025), the fastest-growing analytics product the company has shipped. So the pitch is landing on your desk now, and you're the one who has to say yes or no.

If you're a CFO, a controller, a COO, or the head of data, this is the plain version. You run the business. You don't build the reports. And someone is asking you to sign off on a platform with three new words in it that you can't sanity-check on the spot. Let's fix that.

What does Microsoft Fabric consulting actually cover?

Microsoft Fabric consulting is three jobs, not one.

The first job is the readiness call: should you turn Fabric on yet, or wait. A straight consultant will tell you to wait when your data isn't ready. Fabric on top of messy data just serves you the same wrong numbers faster.

The second job is the setup: connecting your sources, right-sizing the capacity you pay for, and building the reports so people open them instead of exporting to Excel.

The third job is cost control. Fabric bills by capacity, and that number creeps if nobody watches it. Good consulting keeps the monthly cost predictable and tells you before it climbs.

You feel the absence of this work in the moments that matter. A board meeting where sales quotes one revenue figure and finance quotes another, and twenty minutes go to arguing about whose number is right. A month-end close that drags past a week because one person stitches spreadsheets together by hand. A decision made on a figure that turns out to be last quarter's. Gartner puts the average cost of poor data quality at $12.9M a year per organization, and none of it gets fixed by a product name on a quote.

What's actually new with Fabric, in plain terms?

Two things are new, and they're the two words you'll hear in every pitch.

The first is OneLake. It's one shared store for all your company data, so finance, sales, and operations read from the same copy instead of keeping their own. Today most companies do the opposite: everyone keeps a private version, and 82% say data silos get in the way of their work (ZoomInfo). OneLake is Microsoft's answer to that, one place the data lands.

The second is Direct Lake, a faster way for reports to read that shared data. Older setups copy the data into the report first, which works until the data grows and the copy takes too long. Direct Lake reads it straight from OneLake, so reports open fast without the long wait.

The rest of Fabric is mostly older parts you may already own, bundled under one bill. So when a salesperson leads with Fabric, they're often selling you the whole bundle, whether or not you need every piece yet.

When to use Microsoft Fabric, and when should you wait?

The honest test for when to use Microsoft Fabric is whether you've felt a specific pull, or you're just being sold the newest thing.

Turn it on when your data has outgrown the current setup: when refreshes time out, when your engineers and analysts need to read the same large data without copying it around, or when you want the storage, the reporting, and the engineering under one predictable bill. Those are real reasons, and Fabric earns its cost against them.

Wait when your numbers still don't agree across departments. If sales and finance define revenue differently, Fabric won't make them agree, it'll just serve the disagreement faster. Wait, too, if you already own Power BI and it's doing its job on clean data. Only 40% of executives say they always trust their organization's data (Talend), and that gap lives one level below the reporting tool, in the model underneath. Fix that first.

The common mistake is buying the platform to solve a problem the platform doesn't touch. Around 70% of dashboard work goes unused (RevealBI), usually because people don't trust the numbers, and a bigger platform on the same untrusted data changes nothing.

Direct Lake vs import: which one, and does it matter to you?

This is the one technical choice that reaches the business, so here's the plain version of Direct Lake vs import. Both are ways for a report to read your data. The choice decides how fast reports open and how fresh the numbers are.

ImportDirect Lake
What it doesCopies the data into the report to read itReads data straight from OneLake, no copy
When you open a reportFast, once it's loadedFast, with no long refresh to wait on
How fresh the numbers areAs old as the last refresh ranClose to live
Best fitSmaller data, simpler setupLarge data on Fabric you don't want to copy
Watch forRefreshes that time out as data growsNeeds Fabric capacity and clean data underneath

You don't need to make this call yourself. You need to know it exists, and to ask which one your setup uses and why. If the answer is vague, the setup will drift within a quarter. If the answer is specific, someone has thought about how your reports behave when the data doubles.

What should good Microsoft Fabric consulting deliver?

Good work leaves you a short, boring list you can check without knowing how any of it is built. Ask to see each one before you call the project done.

  • One definition per number, so "revenue" means the same thing in the board pack, the sales review, and the finance close.
  • A refresh you can rely on, so the report is current when you open it at 8am, not stuck loading.
  • Access set correctly, so each person sees their own numbers and nothing they shouldn't.
  • The real monthly Fabric cost at your data size, written down, not the sticker price of a single license.
  • Documentation and one training session, so your team can change a number without opening a ticket.

That last point is the one that pays off for years. 94% of business spreadsheets used in decision-making contain errors (Poon et al., 2024), and the whole point of moving to a platform is to get off fragile spreadsheets and onto numbers you can defend in a board meeting. You only keep that result if the trusted setup stays with your team after the consultant leaves.

There's one deeper layer worth naming, kept short. The trust in your numbers gets decided in the data model, not the dashboard. Someone maps every source, agrees one definition per metric, and builds a single clean layer that every report points to. In Fabric that clean layer lives in OneLake, and it's what makes the platform worth its price. Get it right and the arguing stops: one number for revenue, a close that lands in three days instead of eight (SolveXia), and reports the board believes.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need Microsoft Fabric if we already have Power BI? Often not on day one. If your data is clean and Power BI is doing its job, you can wait. Microsoft Fabric earns its cost when your data grows, when refreshes start timing out, or when your engineers and analysts need to read the same large data without copying it around.

What is OneLake in plain terms? OneLake is a single store for your whole company's data, so every team works off the same numbers instead of keeping private versions. It's Microsoft's answer to the silo problem, where finance, sales, and operations each keep their own numbers and none of them agree.

Should we use Direct Lake or import mode? Import copies your data into the report and works well on smaller, simpler setups. Direct Lake reads straight from OneLake and suits large data you don't want to copy. Your consultant should recommend one based on your data size and how fresh the numbers need to be, and explain the choice in plain words.

How much does Microsoft Fabric consulting cost? The consulting fee depends on scope, from a short readiness check to a full setup and handover. Separate from that, Fabric itself bills by capacity, so the running cost depends on your data size. Ask for the monthly capacity figure at your volume up front, so the total is clear before you sign.

Can we start small and move to Fabric later? Yes, and most companies should. Get one clean data source and a handful of trusted reports working in Power BI first. Move to Fabric only when a specific need pushes you there, such as data volume or a combined bill you'd rather have in one place.

Who should own this decision inside the company? One person with authority over the numbers, usually in finance or operations, plus whoever builds the reports. The owner's job is to protect one definition per metric, so the same number means the same thing everywhere, on any platform.
