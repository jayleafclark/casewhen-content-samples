[0:45-2:10] VALUE BEAT 1 - What can I do without paying anything?

CLAIM (on-screen card): "Power BI Desktop is free forever. No trial, no clock."

> "Start with the software. Power BI Desktop is a free download from Microsoft. No trial, no clock counting down, no card on file. You install it on Windows and you get the full authoring tool, the same one paid users open. You can pull in data, clean it, build the model, write DAX measures, and design the whole report. On top of that, Microsoft Learn has official Power BI training for free, the same paths Microsoft uses to prep people for the exam. So the two things you need to start, the tool and the teaching, both cost nothing."

[2:10-3:45] VALUE BEAT 2 - Where does free start costing money?

CLAIM (on-screen card): "Sharing a report needs a paid Pro seat, around $14 per user a month."

> "So, the line. Power BI is free to build with and free to view in your own workspace. Sharing is where it turns paid. The moment you publish a report and a colleague needs to open it, both of you need a Pro seat. Pro runs around fourteen dollars per user a month right now. If you need bigger data or more frequent refresh on your own, there's Premium Per User at roughly twenty-four a month. Above that sits capacity, the Fabric and Premium tier, which is a much bigger commitment. For most people getting started, the number that matters is Pro, per person, on both sides of the report."

(MODALITY: custom animation - THE SHARING WALL. One figure authoring inside a green "Free" box, an arrow labelled "Publish" reaches toward a second figure, a wall drops down stamped "Pro required". Then the animation pulls back into a LICENSE LADDER: Free ($0, build + own view) → Pro (~$14/user/mo, share) → Premium Per User (~$24/user/mo) → Capacity / Fabric (big step up). Each rung lights as it's named.) (ON-SCREEN: takeaway 2 card - "Free = build + your own view. Sharing starts at Pro, per person, both sides.")

PAYOFF: "Free covers building and your own viewing. Sharing and teamwork start at Pro, so budget one paid seat for every person who opens the report, whoever built it."

RE-HOOK: "Software sorted. Now the question I get more than any other. Is the certification free?"

[5:15-6:40] VALUE BEAT 4 - Is Power BI free with Microsoft 365?

CLAIM (on-screen card): "Most Microsoft 365 plans don't include Power BI Pro. Only E5 bundles it."

> "This is the one people get wrong constantly. Paying for Microsoft 365 doesn't mean you have Power BI Pro. Most plans, Business Basic, Business Standard, even the common enterprise E3, give you the free Power BI license only. The plan that bundles Pro is E5, the top enterprise tier. So if your company runs on E3, and plenty do, nobody there can share a Power BI report until someone buys Pro seats."

(MODALITY: custom animation - THE M365 GRID. Five plan cards in a row: Business Basic, Business Standard, Business Premium, Enterprise E3, Enterprise E5. (MODALITY switch: screen-record - the Microsoft 365 admin licensing view, filtering to show who has a Power BI Pro seat assigned) (ON-SCREEN: takeaway 4 card - "Not on E5? You have the free license only. Pro is a separate line item.")

PAYOFF: "Check your exact Microsoft 365 plan before you assume anything. If you're not on E5, you have the free license, and Pro is a separate line on the bill."

RE-HOOK: "Now you've got the tool, the training, and the licensing straight. So why do so many people still stall out after months of free tutorials? Because free content tends to teach one half of the job and skip the half that matters at work."

[6:40-8:20] VALUE BEAT 5 - Why do I keep hitting the same wall?

CLAIM (on-screen card): "Free tutorials teach clicking. The thing that makes a report trustworthy is the model, and they skip it."

> "So why does the wall keep showing up? Most free tutorials walk you through clicking. Drag this field, pick that chart, change the color. That gets you a picture. What they skip is the part underneath, the data model, and that's the part that decides whether your numbers are right. Think about how much rides on this. In a 2021 Experian study, companies believed thirty-two percent of their own data was inaccurate. A prettier chart doesn't fix that. A correct model does. The model that fixes it's the star schema. One central fact table, with dimension tables around it, connected one-to-many. Get that shape right and your measures are simpler, your report is faster, and the totals reconcile."

(MODALITY: data-viz first - the 32% figure counts up on screen with the source line "Experian, 2021" underneath. Then custom animation - SKILL CEILING: a rising line labelled "free tutorials" climbs to a flat plateau marked "make a chart"; a dashed ceiling sits above it, and the space over the ceiling fills with the labels "star schema, DAX, relationships, RLS, refresh". Then the STAR SCHEMA animation from the library: a central fact table, four dimension tables snapping into place around it, one-to-many arrows lighting up.) (ON-SCREEN: takeaway 5 card - "Learn the model before the visuals. Star schema first.")

PAYOFF: "Learn the data model before you touch visuals. A star schema and a few correct measures beat a hundred nicer-looking charts sitting on a messy model."

RE-HOOK: "That ceiling is also the fix. So this is the free path that gets you somewhere."
