How to Migrate From Tableau to Power BI: A Practical Guide

Quick Answer: How to migrate from Tableau to Power BI comes down to four steps: take stock of what you still use, rebuild the data model properly in Power BI, recreate the reports that matter, then run both tools side by side until the numbers match. The mistake most teams make is trying to convert dashboards one for one. Power BI works differently under the hood, so a clean rebuild of the model is faster and safer than a copy.

You picked Tableau years ago, and now the rest of the company runs on Microsoft, so someone has asked how to migrate from Tableau to Power BI without losing every dashboard your team built. That is a fair question and a stressful one. You have dozens of workbooks, a few people who know where the numbers come from, and a licence renewal coming up. Nobody wants to spend six months on a move that leaves the business with two reporting tools and no clear answer to which one is right.

Thousands of teams have done this move, so it is well understood. Most guides still make it sound simpler than it really is. Tableau and Power BI look similar on the surface and think about data very differently underneath. If you treat the move as a straight copy, you get reports that render but return the wrong totals. This guide walks through how to do it properly, in plain terms, without the sales gloss.

What does it really take to migrate from Tableau to Power BI?

Three things move when you switch tools, and they are not equally hard.

The first is the data connections. Tableau connects to your databases, extracts, and files. Power BI does the same, and this part is usually straightforward. You point Power BI at the same sources.

The second is the data model, which is the set of tables, relationships, and calculations behind your charts. This is the real work. Tableau uses its own calculation language and its own way of joining data. Power BI uses a language called DAX and a star-schema model, and the two do not translate line for line. A Tableau calculated field often has to be rethought as a DAX measure, not simply pasted across.

The third is the reports and dashboards themselves, the visuals people open every day. These are the easiest to recreate once the model is right, and the most tempting to start with, which is exactly the trap.

Most teams start with the visuals because that is what everyone can see. Then they discover the numbers do not tie out, because the model underneath was never rebuilt. Start with the model. The visuals come quickly after.

How do you plan a migration without stalling halfway?

The most common way this goes wrong is not technical. A team starts converting workbooks with no priority list, runs out of time or budget, and ends up with half the reports in Power BI and half still in Tableau. Now both licences are being paid for, nobody trusts either version, and the finance team keeps using the old one anyway. The migration stalls half finished and both tools run at once, which is the worst of every outcome.

You avoid that with a short planning pass before anyone touches Power BI.

  1. Inventory what exists. List every Tableau workbook and dashboard. Note who opens each one and how often. Tableau's own usage stats tell you this.
  2. Cut hard. Most Tableau deployments have a long tail of reports nobody opens. You do not migrate those. You retire them. Moving dead reports is pure waste.
  3. Rank what is left. Order the survivors by how much the business relies on them. The month-end pack and the sales pipeline report come first. The one chart someone made in 2021 comes last, if at all.
  4. Pick a first slice. Choose one important, self-contained report to migrate first. You learn the real effort on something that matters, not on a toy.

This step alone often cuts the work in half, because most teams are carrying far more reports than anyone uses.

How to migrate from Tableau to Power BI step by step

Once the plan is set, the build follows a repeatable order. Do it in this sequence and the numbers stay correct.

  • Connect the data. Point Power BI at the same sources Tableau used. Confirm you can see the same raw tables.
  • Rebuild the model as a star schema. Lay out fact tables and dimension tables cleanly, with clear relationships. This is where trusted numbers come from. Do not skip it to save time.
  • Rewrite the calculations as DAX measures. Take each Tableau calculated field that matters and rebuild it as a measure. Write the definition down as you go, so there is one agreed version of revenue, margin, and every other number.
  • Recreate the reports. Now build the visuals. With a clean model, this is the fast part.
  • Add row-level security. So a regional manager sees only their region, the way your Tableau permissions did.
  • Validate against Tableau. Put the old report and the new report next to each other and check that every total matches. Skipping this step is how wrong numbers reach the business.

That validation step is the one people rush, and it is the one that decides whether the business trusts the new tool. If a total is off by even a little, find out why before you switch anyone over.

Should you convert dashboards or rebuild them?

There are tools that promise to convert Tableau workbooks into Power BI automatically. They can help with a first draft and save some clicking, but they will not save you from the real work, because they cannot rebuild a proper data model or decide what your one agreed definition of margin should be. A converted dashboard usually needs almost as much cleanup as a fresh build, and it hides problems inside logic you did not write.

For anything important, rebuild it. The rebuild forces you to decide what each number means, which is the whole point of the exercise. Reserve auto-conversion for low-stakes reports where a rough copy is good enough.

Part of the reason teams take this on at all is cost. Power BI Pro lists at $14 per user each month, against Tableau Creator at $75, according to vendor list prices in 2025. Across a large deployment that gap is real money, and it is often what gets a migration approved. Just do not let the saving tempt you into a rushed copy, because a Power BI report nobody trusts costs far more than the licence you saved.

FAQ

How long does a Tableau to Power BI migration take? It depends far more on how many reports you keep than on the tool. A focused migration of the reports that matter can take a few weeks. A full move of a large, uncleaned Tableau estate can run several months, which is exactly why cutting dead reports first is so important.

Can you automatically convert Tableau dashboards to Power BI? Partly. Some tools generate a first draft, but they cannot rebuild a proper data model or decide what each number means. For important reports, a clean rebuild is faster and safer than fixing an automatic conversion.

Will the numbers match after we migrate from Tableau to Power BI? Only if you validate them. Put the old and new versions side by side and check every total before switching users over. Differences almost always trace back to the data model or a calculation that was copied instead of rebuilt.

Do we have to run Tableau and Power BI at the same time? For a short window, yes, and that is healthy. Running both while you validate lets you prove the new numbers before you retire the old tool. The risk is leaving both running for months with no plan to finish, so set an end date.
