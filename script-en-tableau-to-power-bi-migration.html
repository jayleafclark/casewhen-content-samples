Spoken over animation: "In Tableau a calculated field lives close to the worksheet, and level-of-detail expressions, FIXED, INCLUDE, EXCLUDE, let you pin the grain of a number. A FIXED expression often becomes CALCULATE with ALLEXCEPT. A table calculation such as a running total or a rank becomes a DAX pattern with its own evaluation order. [7:00-8:30] VALUE BEAT 5: Which calculation gives a different answer? CLAIM (spoken, flat): "Percent of total and running totals look the same in both tools and compute differently." (ON-SCREEN: claim card "Same chart, different denominator.") PROOF (MODALITY: custom animation, then talking head): (ANIMATION: the same bar chart drawn twice. On the Tableau side, a quick table calc for percent of total, and the denominator spotlighted as the sum of the visible bars, a box drawn around all the marks on screen. Then show ALL() dropped into the measure and the box expanding back out to all bars, and the two charts finally match.) Spoken over animation: "Here it is. A percent of total. In Tableau you drop a quick table calc and it divides each bar by the sum of the bars you can see. In Power BI, if you write the obvious measure, divide this by the total, DAX reads the total as the current filter context, which is just this bar, so every bar comes back at 100 percent." (MODALITY: talking head) Spoken to camera: "The fix is to tell DAX to ignore the bar's own filter with ALL or ALLSELECTED. There's no import button for a Tableau to Power BI migration and no reliable file converter, so every report is re-authored by hand. Your data sources and SQL move over with little change, but calculated fields become DAX measures with a new filter context, level-of-detail expressions turn into CALCULATE patterns, and table calcs like percent of total need their denominator set explicitly with ALL or ALLSELECTED. The part that decides trust is the model: build one shared star schema and define each measure once, so your numbers stop disagreeing across reports. Scope the work by inventorying workbooks, worksheets, calculated fields and data sources, retire what nobody opens, and size the rest by calculation complexity. Do that and you get a defensible timeline instead of a guess.
