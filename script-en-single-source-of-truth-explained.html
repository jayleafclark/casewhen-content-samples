[0:55-2:25] VALUE BEAT 1 (what it really means)

(MODALITY: custom animation)

CLAIM (on-screen text card): > A single source of truth is one agreed definition of each number that every report reads from.

PROOF (custom animation of the mechanism): > Most people picture one giant database somewhere. That's not it. A single source of truth is an agreement, backed by one place the numbers live. Take the word "revenue". Right now finance might count it after refunds, sales might count it the day the order lands, and marketing might include renewals. Three honest answers, three different totals, all called revenue. A single source of truth means the business decides once what revenue means, that definition gets built one time in one place, and every report, every dashboard, every export pulls from that one place. Nobody redefines it in their own spreadsheet. When someone asks "what was revenue last month", there is one answer, and it's the same answer whoever asks.

[2:25-4:00] VALUE BEAT 2 (why the versions pile up)

(MODALITY: screen-record, real spreadsheets and exports)

PROOF (screen-record the real pattern): > Here's how it happens, and it's nobody's fault. Same report name, four times, with "final", "final v2", and "use this one" in the file names. This is what people mean when they say too many versions of the truth. It's what happens when there's no single agreed place to read from, so everyone builds their own. Around 82 percent of organizations say data silos disrupt their work. Zoominfo put that number on it. And close to 70 percent of dashboard work goes unused, most of it because people don't trust it enough to drop their own copy. That's the RevealBI figure.

[4:00-5:35] VALUE BEAT 3 (what it looks like under the hood, signposted)

(MODALITY: custom animation)

CLAIM (on-screen text card): > Under the hood, a single source of truth is one shared data model where each number is defined a single time.

PROOF (custom animation of the mechanism, plain for a non-builder): > This is the one beat where I go a level deeper. If you don't build reports yourself, you don't need to memorize it. You just need to recognize it when your team says it. The place all the numbers should live has a name: the semantic model, sometimes called the data model. It's the shared layer that sits between your raw data and every report. Your team builds it once. Inside it, they arrange the data into a clean shape, a central table of the events, sales, invoices, orders, surrounded by the things you slice by, date, product, customer, region. Then, and this is the part that matters to you, each headline number, revenue, margin, active customers, gets written one time, inside that model. Every report connects to the model instead of rolling its own math. So when the definition of revenue changes, it changes in one place and every report updates together. When your team says "we need a proper semantic model" or "we should define the measures centrally", this is what they're asking for, and it's the difference between one number and five.

A single source of truth in business intelligence means one agreed definition of each number, built one time in a shared data model, that every report and dashboard reads from. Most mid-sized businesses end up with several versions of the same figure because people export to their own spreadsheets to get their work done, which is why reports disagree and nobody fully trusts the dashboard. This video explains, in plain business terms, what a single source of truth is, why the versions pile up, what the fix looks like under the hood, and what it buys you: a faster month-end close, fewer costly wrong decisions, and a leadership team that trusts the numbers. It also covers why buying another reporting tool usually makes the problem worse, and why one agreed model is what makes Power BI Copilot and other AI assistants answer correctly.
