Chapter 4. Dedicated or serverless SQL, which one am I paying for?

[4:45-5:15] CLAIM Spoken: "The two SQL engines look similar and bill in opposite ways. Dedicated is reserved compute you rent by the hour. Serverless charges you per terabyte of data a query scans." (MODALITY: on-screen text) (ON-SCREEN: card "Dedicated = rent compute by the hour · Serverless = pay per TB scanned")

[5:15-6:10] DEMO Spoken: "Watch the difference on the meter. A dedicated SQL pool is sized in DWUs, and here's the part that surprises finance. The moment you provision it, it's charging you, even overnight when nobody runs a single query. It bills for being switched on, not for being used. You can suspend the pool to stop that meter, but a lot of teams forget, and the bill just runs. Serverless is the opposite shape. There's nothing to provision, you just query files in the lake, and you pay for the gigabytes each query reads. Great for exploring and light workloads, but scan a giant folder ten times a day and per-terabyte pricing adds up fast too. So the choice is about workload, steady heavy reporting leans dedicated, occasional querying over lake files leans serverless." (MODALITY: screen-record) (SCREEN-RECORD: Synapse Studio Manage hub. First open a dedicated SQL pool showing its DWU setting and a "provisioned, running" status, callout: "billing now, query or not." (Visible change every ~6s: cut between the pool settings and the live query footer, two callout boxes)

[6:10-6:25] PAYOFF + RE-HOOK Spoken: "Takeaway four. Chapter 5. What do Spark and Synapse Link actually do?

[6:25-6:55] CLAIM Spoken: "Spark is your heavy-lifting engine for big data prep. Synapse Link is the quiet feature that lets you analyze live operational data without building a single pipeline." (MODALITY: on-screen text) (ON-SCREEN: card "Spark = big transforms · Synapse Link = live data, no ETL")

[6:55-7:50] DEMO Spoken: "Take Spark first. When a file is too big or too messy for SQL, you spin up a Spark pool, write a notebook in Python or Spark SQL, and process millions of rows across a cluster, then land a clean result back in the lake for the SQL engines to read. Standard big-data work, built right into the same workspace. Now Synapse Link, this one's useful and badly underused. Normally, to report on data from an operational database like Cosmos DB or Dataverse or SQL, you'd build pipelines to copy it out on a schedule, which is slow and always a bit stale. Synapse Link mirrors that operational data into an analytical copy in near real time, automatically, with no pipeline to build and no load on the app database. Then Synapse SQL or Spark queries the mirrored copy while your app keeps writing to the original. You get fresh numbers without the copy machinery."
