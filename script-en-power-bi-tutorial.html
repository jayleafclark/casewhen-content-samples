[2:15-3:45] VALUE BEAT 2 - shape the data CLAIM (ON-SCREEN card): "Do all your shaping in Power Query, before the data ever reaches your report." (MODALITY: screen-record) Power Query Editor open on a messy import: a date column stored as text, two stray header rows sitting in the data, a Region column with "North", "north ", and "N" all present. Spoken: "Every beginner hits the same wall. The data comes in messy. Dates that are text. A region column with four spellings of the same region. Totals mixed into the rows. The instinct is to fix it later with a formula in the report. Do it here instead, in Power Query, before the report sees it. Watch. This column is text, I set it to a date. This region field has trailing spaces, I trim it. These two rows aren't data, I remove them. Every step gets recorded on the right, so the same cleanup runs itself on next month's file with zero extra work." (Screen-record beats: click Change Type, then Trim, then Replace Values so the three spellings become one, then Remove Top Rows. The Applied Steps list on the right grows with each click. Cursor spotlight on each step name.) PAYOFF (ON-SCREEN numbered takeaway): "Takeaway 2: Clean in Power Query, not in the report. [3:45-5:20] VALUE BEAT 3 - the model (star schema) CLAIM (ON-SCREEN card): "Build a star schema: one fact table in the middle, dimension tables around it." (MODALITY: custom animation) STAR SCHEMA ANIMATION. Center: a Sales fact table (columns: Date, Product, Customer, Amount) glows in. Around it, four dimension tables snap into place one at a time: Date, Product, Customer, Region. A one-to-many relationship arrow lights up from each dimension into the fact table. Then a contrast beat: a single giant flat table on the left strains and sags under its own width, while the tidy star on the right sits clean. Final beat: a user clicks a filter "Region = North", and a pulse travels down the Region arrow into the fact table, and the total on a card ticks to a smaller number. Spoken: "This is the skill most people learn last, and it's the one that should come third. It's the data model. Instead of one giant flat table with everything crammed into it, you split your data into a fact table, the thing you measure, like sales, and dimension tables around it, like date, product, and customer. You connect them with relationships. That shape is called a star schema. Here's why it earns its place early. When you click a filter on region, that filter flows down these relationships into your numbers on its own, so one measure works across every chart. Learn this third, and every measure and every visual after it gets easier. Learn it last, after you've built forty charts on a flat table, and you rebuild all forty." (Keyword card "star schema" stays on screen through the beat. Slow, quiet delivery on the "learn it third" line; the arrows light up in rhythm with the sentence.) PAYOFF (ON-SCREEN numbered takeaway): "Takeaway 3: Model before you measure. One fact table, dimensions around it, relationships connecting them. This is what makes everything after it easy." RE-HOOK: "Now you've got a clean model. This is where DAX finally earns its reputation, and where most people massively overlearn it."

[5:20-6:55] VALUE BEAT 4 - measures (open-loop payoff) CLAIM (ON-SCREEN card): "Learn ten measures deeply before you learn a hundred." (MODALITY: screen-record) Power BI, measures pane. Create Total Sales = SUM(Sales[Amount]) and show it on a card. Then create Sales North = CALCULATE([Total Sales], Region[Region] = "North"). Then Sales YTD = TOTALYTD([Total Sales], 'Date'[Date]). All three land in a small table, returning numbers. Then drag the same [Total Sales] onto three different visuals to show it being reused. Spoken: "DAX has hundreds of functions, and beginners try to learn all of them. You don't need to. Here's the pattern I promised at the start, the one that answers most business questions. Write one base measure, then reshape it with CALCULATE. Watch. Total Sales is just the sum of amount. That's the base. Now, sales for the north region: I wrap that base measure in CALCULATE and add a filter. Sales year to date: I wrap the same base measure in TOTALYTD. Same base, different wrapper. Once you see that one measure feeds the next, you stop memorizing functions and start composing them. Ten measures learned this way beat a hundred half learned." (Screen-record shows each new measure referencing [Total Sales] by name, so the dependency is visible. Callout box points at the reused [Total Sales] each time it appears.) PAYOFF (ON-SCREEN numbered takeaway): "Takeaway 4: Write one base measure, then reshape it with CALCULATE and time intelligence. Learn ten patterns deeply, not a hundred functions." RE-HOOK: "You've got numbers you trust. Last step, and it's the one everyone starts with and gets backwards. The visuals."

[6:55-8:25] VALUE BEAT 5 - visuals CLAIM (ON-SCREEN card): "Pick the visual from the question, not from the gallery." Spoken: "Visuals are where everyone begins, and it's the part worth learning last, because a chart is only as good as the model under it. When you do get here, one rule covers most of it. Start from the question, then pick the chart. Change over time is a line. Ranking, like top products, is a bar, sorted. Parts of a whole, once in a while, is a bar again, because a pie chart with twelve slices tells you nothing. You don't need thirty chart types. Learn about five, learn when each one answers a question, and skip the decorative ones." (Fast montage on the wrong charts; slow beat on the two right charts as the green checks land.) PAYOFF (ON-SCREEN numbered takeaway): "Takeaway 5: Choose the chart from the question. Change over time is a line, ranking is a sorted bar. Learn five charts, not thirty." Most Power BI tutorials teach the steps in the wrong order, so beginners spend months on formatting and chart colors and almost no time on the data model that everything depends on. This video walks the order that works: get data, shape it in Power Query, model it as a star schema, write a small set of DAX measures, then choose visuals from the question you're answering. It closes with a one week plan that maps each day to one step, plus the base measure plus CALCULATE pattern that answers most business questions. If you're learning Power BI or getting a team up to speed, this is the sequence to follow.
