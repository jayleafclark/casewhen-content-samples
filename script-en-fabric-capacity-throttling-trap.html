CLAIM (AUSTIN, on camera, flat): "Your capacity is measured in capacity units, and every query and every refresh spends capacity-unit seconds. An F64 gives you sixty-four of those units a second. That's the budget." (MODALITY: talking head → cut to screen-record) (ON-SCREEN: claim card: "You buy CU per second. Every operation spends CU-seconds.")

(MODALITY: custom animation) (ON-SCREEN: claim card: "Smoothing spreads an operation's CU cost over time.")

[3:50-5:25] VALUE BEAT 3 - So when does throttling really kick in? CLAIM (AUSTIN, voice over): "Throttling runs on how much future capacity you've already borrowed. There are three lines, and each one does something worse than the last." (MODALITY: data-viz) (ON-SCREEN: claim card: "Throttling is based on borrowed future capacity, in 3 stages.")

PROOF / DEMO (MODALITY: data-viz): Animated three-stage bar. A single meter labeled "carry-forward overage" fills left to right with three marked thresholds. PAYOFF (AUSTIN, voice over): "Takeaway three, and this is the one to memorize: under ten minutes of overage you're fine, past sixty minutes your users get errors, and past twenty-four hours even your refreshes get rejected. [8:40-10:25] FIXES - How do I stop reports going offline? CLAIM (AUSTIN, on camera): "Four moves, cheapest first, and you do the first three before you ever pay for more capacity." (MODALITY: talking head) (ON-SCREEN: claim card: "4 fixes, cheapest first.")

PROOF / DEMO (MODALITY: talking head with on-screen list building): Austin to camera, each fix pops onto a numbered list as he says it, with a small b-roll cutaway per item. - "One: move heavy refreshes off-peak. (cutaway: a refresh schedule dialog moving from 9am to 3am) - "Two: cut the cost of the refresh at the source. Trim columns you don't use, turn on incremental refresh so you only reload recent data, and fix the slow queries in the semantic model. (cutaway: incremental refresh setup) - "Three: turn on capacity notifications in the admin portal so Fabric emails you when throttling starts, instead of your users telling you." (cutaway: the notification settings screen) - "Four, and only after the first three: right-size the capacity. Scale the SKU up if the numbers really need it, or scale it down and stop it from Azure on weekends to control the cost. Sizing is the last lever, not the first." Fabric capacity throttling catches teams out because the overview gauge shows a smoothed average, not the carry-forward overage that decides whether reports respond. This video explains capacity units and CU-seconds, how interactive and background smoothing work, the three throttling stages at ten minutes, sixty minutes, and twenty-four hours, where to read them in the Fabric Capacity Metrics app, and the four fixes in cheapest-first order: move refreshes off-peak, lighten the refresh, turn on capacity notifications, and right-size the SKU last. If your Power BI and Fabric reports go down while the capacity looks fine, this is how you find and stop it.
