[2:05-3:40] VALUE BEAT 2 : Where exactly is the price cliff? CLAIM (ON-SCREEN card): "The cliff is the free-viewer threshold. It has a name: F64." Spoken: "The jump happens the day you want viewers who don't each have a paid seat. In per-user land, every single person who opens a report needs a Pro or PPU licence. The moment you want a hundred people, or the whole company, to read a dashboard without buying a hundred seats, you have to move to capacity. And Microsoft draws one hard line here. Free viewing for users without their own licence only starts at the F64 capacity, or the older P1 it replaces. Below F64, no free viewers. At F64, everyone in your tenant can read the reports on that capacity. So your options are a per-seat bill that keeps climbing with every viewer, or one capacity that starts near five thousand dollars a month. There's very little in between. An analyst counts the viewers, sees the per-seat total creeping up, and asks the obvious question. The answer comes back as a five-thousand-dollar step up." [3:40-5:05] VALUE BEAT 3 : How many viewers before capacity is cheaper? CLAIM (ON-SCREEN card): "The break-even is roughly 350 to 500 viewers." Spoken: "Let's do the arithmetic on screen. One capacity that gives free viewing, an F64 on a one-year reservation, runs about five thousand dollars a month. Pro seats are about fourteen dollars each. Five thousand divided by fourteen is around three hundred and sixty. So if every one of your viewers would otherwise need a Pro seat, capacity starts winning somewhere around three hundred and fifty to five hundred viewers, once you factor in the authors who still need their own Pro or PPU seats to publish. Under that count, per-user is cheaper. Over it, capacity is cheaper and simpler to administer. The trap is the middle. Plenty of teams flip to a five-thousand-dollar capacity at eighty viewers because they wanted the free viewing, when eighty Pro seats would have cost them around eleven hundred dollars. They bought around four thousand dollars of monthly headroom to answer a licensing preference, when the seats alone would have cost a fraction of that." [5:05-6:40] VALUE BEAT 4 : Why is my bill double the sticker price? CLAIM (ON-SCREEN card): "Pay-as-you-go F64 is about $8,400. The reservation is about $5,000." Spoken: "There are two ways to buy the same F64 capacity. Pay-as-you-go, billed by the hour, comes to roughly eighty-four hundred dollars a month if you leave it running around the clock. A one-year reservation for the identical capacity is about five thousand. Same compute, same performance, a difference of about three thousand four hundred dollars every month, purely on how you committed to it. Most teams switch Fabric on with a pay-as-you-go capacity to try it, forget it's running twenty-four seven, and get a first invoice near eighty-four hundred instead of the five thousand they'd budgeted. Then there's the second surge. Fabric smooths your compute usage over time, so a heavy morning refresh gets averaged across the day. When you consistently ask for more than the F64 gives you, two things happen. If you left autoscale on, it bills the extra on top. If you didn't, the capacity throttles, and reports slow down or stop responding while the metrics still read green as though everything's fine. So the invoice grows for load you can't even see on the summary screen." [6:40-8:30] VALUE BEAT 5 : How do I cap the cost before it hits? CLAIM (ON-SCREEN card): "Five guardrails cap Power BI capacity cost." Spoken: "Five things, and you can set most of them this week. One, buy the reservation, not pay-as-you-go, the day you know the capacity is staying. That alone takes about three thousand four hundred a month off the top. Two, install the Microsoft Fabric Capacity Metrics app. It's free, and it shows you the compute units you're actually using, so you can right-size instead of guessing. Two-thirds of the teams we look at are paying for an F64 while using a fraction of it. Three, set a lower F-SKU if the metrics support it. An F32 or F16 costs proportionally less, and if you don't need free viewing for the whole tenant, you may not need F64 at all. Four, turn the capacity off when nobody's using it. A capacity you switch off overnight and on weekends can cut a pay-as-you-go bill by more than half, and you can automate the on-and-off schedule. Five, watch autoscale. Leaving it wide open is how a predictable five-thousand-dollar line becomes an unpredictable one. Cap it, or switch it off and let throttling be the signal that you've outgrown the tier."
