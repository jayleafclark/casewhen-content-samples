5 Signs You Need a Power BI Consultant (Before the Next Board Meeting)

Quick answer. The clearest signs you need a Power BI consultant are slow dashboards that won't refresh, two reports that disagree on the same number, and a team still exporting everything to Excel. When those show up together, the tool isn't the problem, the setup underneath it is, and that's the point to bring in outside help. ---

The clearest signs you need a Power BI consultant don't show up in a report. They show up in a meeting, where two people are reading the same metric off two screens and getting two different answers. You bought the software. You have dashboards. And you still can't walk into a board review sure that the number on the slide is the right one.

If that sounds familiar, you're not behind. This is the most common thing operators and finance leads call us about. The reports look modern, the tool is fine, and the work still limps along underneath. Below are the five signs that tell you it's time to get help, why each one happens in plain terms, what a working setup looks like from your seat, and how to make that call without gambling on it.

What are the signs you need a Power BI consultant?

Here are the five that matter. One on its own might just be a busy week. Two or three together mean the foundation needs work, not another dashboard.

  • Your dashboards are slow, or the refresh won't finish right before you need it. You open the report ten minutes before a meeting and it's still loading last week's data, or it's throwing an error. Power BI refresh failures are usually the first sign the plumbing underneath was never built to carry the load.
  • Two reports give two different numbers for the same thing. Sales says revenue is one figure, finance says another, and the meeting turns into an argument about whose number is right instead of what to do next.
  • Your team still exports everything to Excel. You paid for a reporting tool, and the version that actually gets emailed around is a spreadsheet somebody rebuilt by hand the night before.
  • Every number goes through one person, and you wait days for it. You ask a simple question and it joins a queue. If that person is on holiday, the answer stops.
  • Nobody fully trusts the numbers, so calls get made on gut. When people doubt the report, they stop using it, and the last real decision was made on a hunch.

Only 40% of executives say they always trust their organization's data (Talend). If your own reporting sits in the other 60%, these signs are how it shows up day to day.

Why does this keep happening?

Because in most companies the tool got installed on top of messy data, and nobody went back to fix the data. A dashboard reads from whatever it's pointed at. Point it at five spreadsheets that already disagree, and you get a nicer-looking version of the same disagreement.

The slow refresh is the same story. When a report has to pull raw data from a dozen places and stitch it together every time it opens, it drags, and eventually it breaks. A setup built the right way loads that data once, cleanly, and serves it fast. Most reports that struggle were never built that way, they just grew.

And the Excel habit isn't your team being difficult. People trust a spreadsheet because they can click a cell and see how the number was made. A dashboard that shows a total with no way to trace it gets exported so someone can pull it apart by hand. Around 70% of dashboard work is wasted because people don't trust or use what gets built (RevealBI). That waste is the export habit, made visible.

What does good reporting actually look like?

From your seat, good is simple to describe. You ask for a number and you get one answer. The report opens fast, it's already current, and you can see how the figure was worked out without opening a spreadsheet.

Underneath, that comes from one shared model. Revenue is defined once, in one place, and every report reads from that same definition. Sales, finance, and the board pack all show the same figure by default. That's what a single source of truth means in plain terms. It's one agreed set of rules for what each number is and where it comes from.

When that exists, the signs go away on their own. The refresh runs on a schedule and finishes before you're awake. Nobody exports to Excel to double-check, because the report is already the trusted copy. And the board pack reconciles, because there's only one version of each number to begin with.

What should you ask for before you call anyone?

You don't need to learn Power BI to fix this. You need to ask for the right things and recognize a good answer when you hear one. Start here.

  1. Ask who owns each core number. Revenue, margin, cash, each defined once, with a named person responsible. If nobody can tell you who owns the definition of revenue, that's the first thing to fix.
  2. Ask why the refresh is slow or won't finish. A good answer names a cause, like too much raw data loading every time, and a plan to load it once and keep it current on a schedule.
  3. Ask where each number is defined. The answer should be one place, not "it depends which report." If it depends, you don't have one source of truth yet.
  4. Ask what it would take to stop the Excel exports. Usually the honest answer is a proper model plus the ability for each manager to filter to their own slice inside the report, so they stop keeping private copies.

### What your team actually does about it

One level deeper, and this is the part to hand off rather than run yourself. The fix is a proper data model sitting under the reports, a clean structure where each business fact links to things like date, product, and region, with the core numbers written once as measures. Your team builds that model, moves the shared definitions into it, sets a scheduled refresh so the data loads cleanly instead of choking on every open, and turns on row-level security so each manager sees their own view of the same report. From then on, every report reads the same numbers and opens fast. It's real work, and it's usually the work nobody has spare hours for while also running the monthly close. That's the gap an outside pair of hands fills.

When should you hire a Power BI consultant instead of fixing it in-house?

Both paths are valid. The question is honest capacity and time. If you have a strong data person who owns this and has months of clear runway, in-house can work. If your one analyst is already the bottleneck, or the problem has to be solved before your next quarter close, waiting for spare time is the expensive option. Knowing when to hire a BI consultant is really about which risk you'd rather carry.

When a real choice rides on a hand-built export, the cost of getting it wrong is usually larger than the cost of getting it fixed properly.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I actually need a Power BI consultant or just a better report? Look at whether the problem is one report or the foundation. A single ugly chart is a report fix. Slow refreshes, numbers that disagree, and a team stuck in Excel are foundation problems, and those are the signs you need a Power BI consultant rather than another dashboard.

Why does our Power BI refresh keep breaking or running slow? Usually because the report pulls too much raw data from too many places every time it opens. A proper setup loads the data once, cleans it, and keeps it current on a schedule, so the report opens fast and stays stable under load.

We already have Power BI. Why does finance still work in Excel? Because the model underneath was never built or agreed on. The dashboard shows totals people can't trace, so they export to Excel to check the workings by hand. Fix the model and the trust moves back to the report.

How long should this take to fix? A focused engagement usually gets you to a trusted core set of numbers in weeks, not months, because the scope is fixed and it's the only thing the team is doing. Doing it in-house between other work is what stretches it out.

How long should month-end close take? High performers close in under three days, while around eight is what most companies accept (Solvexia). If yours runs longer because someone stitches exports together each cycle, the manual setup is the reason, not the tool.
