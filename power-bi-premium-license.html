Power BI Premium License: No Sales Pitch, Just the Math

Quick answer. A power bi premium license is one of two things: Premium Per User at $24 per person each month, or a Fabric capacity that starts near $5,000 a month and lets your whole company view reports with no paid seat. Which one is cheaper depends entirely on how many people only read reports. --- A power bi premium license means two very different things depending on who is buying, and mixing them up is where budgets go wrong. Premium Per User costs $24 per person each month. A full Fabric capacity, the option Microsoft used to sell as Power BI Premium, starts near $5,000 a month. Same word on the price page, two answers that sit thousands of dollars apart. Before you sign anything, you need to know which one your team is really being quoted.

This matters because the wrong choice shows up somewhere people notice. A controller signs off on a rollout, the invoice lands three months later, and the number is triple what the demo suggested. Or a board pack goes out with a report only half the room can open, because the viewers were never licensed. Poor data quality already costs organizations an average of $12.9M a year (Gartner). A licensing mistake sits right on top of that, and it is far easier to prevent.

What is a Power BI Premium license, really?

For years, Power BI had two Premium products. Premium Per User was a per-person seat. Premium capacity, sold as P SKUs, was a fixed monthly block of compute for a whole organization. In 2025 Microsoft folded Premium capacity into Microsoft Fabric, so the P SKUs are being retired and the capacity option is now a Fabric F SKU.

That leaves two things a salesperson might call a power bi premium license today. One is Premium Per User, still a seat at $24 a month. The other is a Fabric capacity, the big-team block that used to carry the Premium name. When someone quotes you Premium, your first question is simple: per person, or per capacity? The answer changes the bill by an order of magnitude.

What does a Power BI Premium license cost per month?

Premium Per User is $24 per person each month. For a small group of heavy builders, that is the whole story: ten people who need the bigger engine cost $240 a month, or $2,880 a year.

A Fabric capacity is priced by the block, not by the head. An F64, the entry point that gives free viewing to everyone in the company, lists near $5,000 a month on a one-year reservation, and there is no per-viewer charge on top. So the number that matters is never the sticker price alone, it is the price divided by how many people it covers.

How is Premium different from Power BI Pro cost?

The Power BI Pro cost is $14 per user each month, and Pro already covers most work: build, publish, share, and read, as long as everyone who touches the report also has Pro. Microsoft raised Pro from $10 to $14 in 2025, its first increase in about a decade, and moved Premium Per User from $20 to $24 in the same change.

Premium Per User buys more room for the same work. A Pro dataset caps at 1 GB and eight scheduled refreshes a day. Premium Per User lifts that to a 100 GB model and 48 refreshes a day, and adds paginated reports and deployment pipelines. If a report drags because the model is large or the numbers need to refresh every hour, the $24 seat is the cheaper move before you consider a capacity.

When should you buy Premium instead of more Pro seats?

There are two moments to move up.

The first is engine. If a handful of builders keep hitting the 1 GB Pro ceiling or need hourly refreshes, put those specific people on Premium Per User at $24. You upgrade the people who hit the limit, not the whole team.

The second is viewers. Once a large group only reads dashboards and never builds, per-seat pricing turns expensive. Say 400 people need to open a daily report. On Pro that is $5,600 a month just to look. A Fabric F64 at about $5,000 covers all of them under one bill, and every viewer opens the report free. The break-even sits near 350 viewers: below that, per-seat Pro is cheaper; above it, a capacity wins. Run your own headcount through both numbers before you commit.

What does Microsoft Power BI licensing include beyond Premium?

Microsoft Power BI licensing is really three groups of people wearing three price tags. Builders need Desktop, which is free, plus a seat to publish. Sharers and viewers need Pro or a capacity behind them. Heavy builders and big audiences are where Premium and Fabric come in.

Two costs never show on the price page. The first is admin time. Someone has to manage workspaces, refresh schedules, and access, and that grows with every new report. The second is trust. 67% of organizations say they do not fully trust the data they use to make decisions (Precisely), and 94% of business spreadsheets used in decisions contain errors (Poon et al.). A cheaper license does nothing about either. The point of paying for the right tier is a set of numbers the board believes, on a close that takes days rather than weeks. High performers close the month in under three days; the average drags past eight (Solvexia). A seat is small next to that.

Frequently asked questions

Is Power BI Premium still a product in 2026? The name lives on in two places. Premium Per User is still a $24 per-person seat. Premium capacity, the old P SKUs, is being retired and folded into Microsoft Fabric, so the big-team option is now a Fabric capacity. If someone quotes you Premium, ask whether they mean per user or per capacity.

How much is a Power BI Premium license per user? Premium Per User is $24 per person each month. That covers everything Pro does plus a 100 GB model, 48 daily refreshes, paginated reports, and deployment pipelines. You only need it for people who outgrow the 1 GB Pro limit.

Is Premium Per User worth it over the Power BI Pro cost? It depends on the workload. If your models are small and refresh overnight, the $14 Pro seat is enough. Premium Per User earns its extra $10 only when a report needs a bigger model or more frequent refresh. Upgrade the people who hit the limit, not the whole team.

When does a Fabric capacity beat per-seat licensing? Around 350 viewers. Below that, licensing each reader on Pro at $14 is cheaper than a $5,000 capacity. Above it, one capacity covers unlimited viewers for less than the pile of seats. Count only the people who read reports and never build.

Do report viewers need their own license? Under per-seat pricing, yes. Every person who opens a report, even a board member who looks once a month, needs a Pro or Premium Per User seat. A Fabric capacity removes that, since viewers open reports with no paid seat, which is why big audiences push teams toward a capacity.
